# Blockchain-based voluntary carbon market: strategic insights into network structure

## Citation

Bassi, Eleonóra, Michael Lustenberger, and Srebrenka Letina. "Blockchain-based voluntary carbon market: strategic insights into network structure". *Frontiers in Blockchain*, Vol. 8, 2025-07-21. https://doi.org/10.3389/fbloc.2025.1603695.

## One-line summary

Network analysis of the blockchain-based voluntary carbon market finds a sparse, fragmented ecosystem where legacy tokenisation approaches constrained by Verra's prohibition face structural disadvantages compared to native blockchain tokenisation models.

## Key claims

- The blockchain-based VCM ecosystem displays low network density (0.04) and eight separate connected components, indicating significant fragmentation rather than consolidation.
- Companies using legacy tokenisation methods (bridging Verra-issued VCUs onto blockchains) show lower rates of partnership formation with other legacy players, reflecting Verra's prohibition as a structural constraint on the sector.
- Shared blockchain platform usage correlates weakly with partnership formation (QAP correlation: 0.11, p < 0.0001), suggesting technological alignment alone does not drive ecosystem collaboration.
- Native tokenisation models, which issue carbon credits directly on-chain, enable shorter supply chains by consolidating functions that legacy approaches distribute across multiple parties.
- Four strategic network roles emerge: key hubs (Toucan, KlimaDAO), strategic bridges, local connectors, and peripheral nodes.
- Shared network neighbours are the strongest predictor of new partnership formation, outweighing standardisation choices or blockchain platform selection.

## Significance

- The study provides the first systematic network analysis of how Verra's crypto policy has shaped the structure of the blockchain-based carbon credit market, confirming that the prohibition has fragmented legacy tokenisation actors.
- The fragmentation finding supports Verra's 2025 pivot toward digital infrastructure (Hedera, S&P Global registry): without Verra enabling tokenisation, a parallel native-token ecosystem grows outside its oversight.
- The dominance of Toucan and KlimaDAO as hubs shows the market filled the vacuum left by Verra's caution, with regulatory implications for how standards bodies engage with tokenised carbon credit platforms.
- Network structure evidence is directly relevant to the double-counting and fraud risks Verra cited in its 2022 consultation: fragmentation makes systemic monitoring harder.
