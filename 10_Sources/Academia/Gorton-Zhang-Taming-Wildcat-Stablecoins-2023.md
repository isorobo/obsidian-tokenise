# Taming Wildcat Stablecoins

## Citation

Gorton, Gary B., and Jeffery Y. Zhang. "Taming Wildcat Stablecoins". University of Chicago Law Review 90(3), pp. 909-965, 2023 (page metadata dated 30 April 2023). https://lawreview.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/2023-04/03_Zhang%20%26%20Gorton_ART_Final.pdf.

## One-line summary

Gorton and Zhang argue that stablecoins are circulating private money like nineteenth-century banknotes, that issuers are unregulated banks exposed to runs, and that the options are insured-bank issuance, one-for-one safe-asset backing or a central bank digital currency.

## Key claims

- Stablecoins recreate circulating private money, so their issuers function as unregulated banks.
- Privately issued money fails as a medium of exchange when it is not reliably accepted at par, which undermines the no-questions-asked property that money needs.
- Stablecoin issuers face bank-style run risk, and the 2008 and 2020 money market fund runs show how runs spread into credit markets.
- Current US law leaves unclear whether stablecoins count as deposits or issuers as banks, and the authors argue that the Justice Department's 1979 reasoning could support treating many as deposits.
- Options for reducing risk are issuance through insured banks, one-for-one backing with safe assets, or a central bank digital currency, and each has trade-offs.
- The National Banking Era shows that backing private money with Treasuries led to underissuance and a shift to demand deposits, a caution for policymakers.
- Waiting is risky because stablecoins could grow into a systemically important industry.
