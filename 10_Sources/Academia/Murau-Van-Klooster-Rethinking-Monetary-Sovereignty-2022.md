# Rethinking Monetary Sovereignty: The Global Credit Money System and the State

## Citation

Murau, Steffen, and Jens van 't Klooster. "Rethinking Monetary Sovereignty: The Global Credit Money System and the State". Perspectives on Politics 21(4), pp. 1319-1336, December 2023 (published online 29 August 2022, CC BY 4.0). https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/148148998/rethinking-monetary-sovereignty-the-global-credit-money-system-and-the-state.pdf. DOI 10.1017/S153759272200127X.

## One-line summary

Murau and van 't Klooster replace the Westphalian view of monetary sovereignty (issuing your own currency) with an effective view, a state's ability to use monetary governance tools to reach its policy goals across public, regulated private and unregulated private money.

## Key claims

- The Westphalian view of monetary sovereignty, control over one's own currency, rests on an outdated picture of the monetary system.
- Public money is a small share of the money supply. Most money is private credit money such as bank deposits and shadow money, and much of it is created offshore in foreign units of account, above all the US dollar.
- Effective monetary sovereignty is defined as a state's ability to use its monetary governance tools to reach its economic policy goals.
- Governance works differently across three segments: public money (central bank design), regulated private-public money (banks), and private money (ban, accommodate or leave alone).
- The Westphalian view is criticised for focusing on public onshore money, hiding states' unequal positions, overstating the importance of issuing a currency (the euro example) and building in specific policy prescriptions.
- The effective conception is policy-neutral, so it can compare states such as the US, Bangladesh and Denmark, and it could support a monetary sovereignty index.

- Supplies the vault's main theory source on monetary sovereignty, framed around private credit money and offshore dollar money rather than state-issued currency.
