# Tokenizing Real-World Assets

## Citation

Cong, Lin William, Simon Mayer, and Daniel Rabetti. "Tokenizing Real-World Assets". WIFPR White Paper, Wharton Initiative on Financial Policy and Regulation, University of Pennsylvania, May 2026. https://wifpr.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/WIFPR-Tokenizing-Real-World-Assets-Cong-Mayer-and-Rabetti.pdf.

## One-line summary

The paper proposes the speed-matching principle -- that a tokenised claim should trade no faster than its underlying asset can be valued or redeemed -- and builds a policy framework around it, tying regulatory requirements to the economic role and liquidity of underlying markets rather than to the technology.

## Key claims

- RWA tokenisation distinguishes three asset categories: tokenised liquid assets (gold, equities), money-like claims (stablecoins, tokenised deposits), and tokenised illiquid assets (loans, private credit, real estate).
- The speed-matching principle holds that the trading speed of a tokenised claim must match the speed at which the underlying can be traded, valued, or redeemed, to prevent secondary-market fragility.
- Tokenisation of liquid assets primarily serves integration into on-chain ecosystems and programmability, not liquidity creation; tokenisation of illiquid assets resembles securitisation and inherits its incentive problems.
- Tokenisation changes the trading venue, investor base, and settlement speed but does not alter an asset's cash flows or risk profile.
- The policy framework prioritises clear legal foundations, credible redemption mechanisms, robust custody and audit standards, and oracle governance, rather than technology-specific rules.
- Stablecoins and tokenised money-market fund shares serve analogous roles as on-chain media of exchange and units of account, and warrant similar regulatory treatment.

> "making such claims tradable does not, on its own, make either the claims or the underlying assets genuinely liquid. Tradability does not imply active trading"

> "This liquidity transformation inherits incentive problems familiar from banking and securitization, while introducing new economic and operational risks around custody, redemption design, and oracles."
> ~ Abstract

> "tokenized money market fund shares may serve a similar role under appropriate regulation. In this sense, tokenized liquid assets play a role analogous to stablecoins."

## Significance

- Introduces the speed-matching principle as the central organising idea for RWA regulation, providing the wiki with a doctrinal anchor for comparing regulatory frameworks across jurisdictions.
- Distinguishes stablecoins and tokenised deposits from illiquid RWAs in a policy-relevant way, supporting the wiki's cross-domain analysis of monetary policy and market infrastructure.
- Addresses custody, redemption design, and oracle governance as the key operational risks unique to tokenised assets, extending beyond the standard securities-law lens.
- Commissioned by WIFPR and authored by senior finance faculty at NTU, CMU, and NUS; carries significant weight in policy and academic discourse on RWA regulation.
