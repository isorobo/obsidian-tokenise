# A Taxonomy of Real-World Asset Tokenization for Blockchain-Based Financial Infrastructure

## Citation

Vella, Giorgio, Luca Pennella, and Mark C. Ballandies. "A Taxonomy of Real-World Asset Tokenization for Blockchain-Based Financial Infrastructure". arXiv:2606.08534 (econ.GN; cs.CY), 7 June 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08534.

## One-line summary

The paper classifies twenty major RWA tokenisation systems across 23 dimensions, finding that all rely on hybrid architectures where on-chain tokens handle transfer and composability while legal guarantees remain anchored off-chain.

## Key claims

- Current RWA tokenisation uses hybrid architecture: blockchain handles representation, transfer control, redemption workflows, and composability, while custody, legal enforceability, and compliance stay off-chain.
- The authors organise 23 system dimensions into five components: governance, asset structure, token properties, distributed ledger technology, and economy.
- The 20 studied protocols concentrate in financial assets, particularly fixed-income instruments, money-market funds, and Treasury-bill strategies.
- Primary market access remains permissioned despite deployment on public blockchains; KYC, whitelisting, and qualified-investor requirements are standard.
- Significant documentation gaps exist across protocols for voting rights, dispute forums, burn mechanics, supply caps, and reserve verification.
- Leading protocols operate under issuer-administered conventional legal entities, not decentralised autonomous organisations.

## Significance

- Provides the first systematic taxonomy of RWA tokenisation covering 23 dimensions across 20 major protocols, enabling rigorous comparison across asset classes and implementation models.
- Demonstrates that full on-chain settlement of legal claims is not yet achieved by any major protocol, which has direct bearing on how the wiki analyses custody, intermediation, and transfer doctrine.
- Identifies documentation gaps (voting rights, reserve verification, supply mechanics) that regulators and researchers must address before RWA markets can be adequately supervised.
- Grounds the wiki's corpus in a shared analytical vocabulary for classifying tokenised instruments by governance, asset structure, and DLT layer.
