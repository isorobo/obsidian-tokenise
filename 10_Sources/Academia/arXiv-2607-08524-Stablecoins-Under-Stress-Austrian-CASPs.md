# Stablecoins under Stress in a National Economy: Transaction-Level Evidence from Austrian Crypto-Asset Service Providers

## Citation

Saggese, Pietro, Michael Sigmund, Burkhard Raunig, Esther Segalla, Bernhard Haslhofer, and Christos A. Makridis. "Stablecoins under Stress in a National Economy: Transaction-Level Evidence from Austrian Crypto-Asset Service Providers". arXiv:2607.08524 (q-fin.GN, cs.CR), 9 July 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08524.

## One-line summary

Using a regulatory registry that directly identifies every Austrian crypto-asset service provider's on-chain addresses, the paper finds stablecoins do not act as a uniform safe haven during stress, and that USDC's two-tiered redemption mechanism forces retail holders to sell below par while institutions redeem at par.

## Key claims

- Austrian crypto-asset service providers (CASPs) intermediate roughly $30 billion in on-chain flows with external counterparties, but domestic intermediation between Austrian CASPs totals just $5.8 million, so the ecosystem is integrated globally rather than domestically.
- A further 41.1% of total on-chain volume reflects within-entity bookkeeping rather than genuine counterparty activity, and would be misclassified as economic activity in aggregate on-chain data.
- External activity is highly concentrated: counterparties transacting through CASP warm and cold wallets account for 57.8% of external volume while representing only 0.77% of external transfers.
- Retail-like and institutional actors respond to systemic shocks through different mechanisms; the FTX collapse drove persistent retail-like withdrawals to self-custodial wallets, while institutional flows reflected cross-platform liquidity rebalancing rather than a one-way exit.
- The Silicon Valley Bank failure produced a narrower response concentrated in USDC, the stablecoin with direct Circle reserve exposure, and did not propagate to Bitcoin or Ether.
- Stablecoins do not act as a systematic within-crypto safe haven; responses to the Terra-Luna, FTX, and SVB shocks were two-sided and shock-specific rather than a uniform flight-to-quality inflow.
- USDC's asymmetric redemption mechanism restricts par redemption to institutions, forcing retail investors to trade below par in secondary markets during stress, a structural disadvantage most visible during the SVB episode.

## Significance

- Supplies the first registry-based, transaction-level measurement of a national jurisdiction's cryptoasset intermediation, offering a reproducible method regulators can replicate elsewhere.
- Directly tests and rejects the stablecoin-as-safe-haven hypothesis with address-level evidence, refining the wiki's singleness-of-money and reserve-requirements analysis of stablecoin design.
- Documents a concrete distributional harm in USDC's redemption architecture, relevant to consumer-protection debates feeding into MiCA and GENIUS Act supervisory practice.
- Shows that 41.1% of aggregate on-chain volume is internal bookkeeping, a methodological caution for any wiki source relying on raw chain-level stablecoin flow statistics.
