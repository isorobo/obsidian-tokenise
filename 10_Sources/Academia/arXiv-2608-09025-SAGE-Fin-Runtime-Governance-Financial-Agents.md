# Context Is Not Authority: Structured Runtime Governance for Financial Market Agents

## Citation

Tang, Rui, Qiangqiang Liu, Yichi Zhang, Youwei Wang, Xi Chen, and Chen Dong. "Context Is Not Authority: Structured Runtime Governance for Financial Market Agents". arXiv:2608.09025 (cs.AI), 10 August 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09025.

## One-line summary

The paper presents SAGE-Fin, a runtime governance contract that controls the proposed effect of a financial agent's action, not just its generated text, before execution.

## Key claims

- Financial agents can turn correct context into an unauthorised effect, such as a customer-facing commitment, a trade, or a deployed policy.
- SAGE-Fin compiles proposals into typed, adapter-bound candidates and records missing or stale institutional obligations as coverage debt.
- The system contracts authority under current market, account, policy, and dialogue state, and requires an exact-artifact receipt matching the consuming adapter.
- Evidence and workflow progress cannot substitute for effect authority, and prior authorisation is rechecked after any state change.
- Across an authored 616-case catalog, five deterministic specifications yield 3,080 outputs, and a label-isolated harness obtains 616 of 616 binary reference-prototype parity.
- SAGE-Fin's response gate processed real customer-facing production requests at a confidential digital-asset platform, where an independent operational team reached a strongly positive post-deployment conclusion.
- Three de-identified predecessor failures, each independently confirmed as 0 of 3 interceptions, motivate the design: repeated-emission drift, stale account evidence, and missing escalation state.

## Significance

- Gives the corpus a concrete runtime-control architecture for financial agents, moving the agentic-AI governance thread from disclosure-gap evidence toward a deployable technical control.
- Reports a production deployment at a digital-asset platform, one of the few sources in the channel with field evidence of agentic governance operating on a live tokenised or crypto-asset system.
- Names three specific failure modes, repeated-emission drift, stale account evidence, and missing escalation state, that a supervisory approach to agentic trading systems would need to test for.
- Complements the AI Governance for Institutional Readiness paper already indexed by shifting from a disclosure-based governance gap to an executable authority-control mechanism.
