# Authority-Inference Separation in Agentic Finance: First-Line Control, Blockchain Enforcement, and Replayable Assurance

## Citation

Gong, Hui; Samawi, Michail; Medda, Francesca. "Authority-Inference Separation in Agentic Finance: First-Line Control, Blockchain Enforcement, and Replayable Assurance". arXiv (q-fin.GN), 31 August 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30519.

## One-line summary

The paper proposes an architecture in which an AI agent's financial action gains executable authority only after an independent deterministic control plane validates it, with blockchain enforcing and recording settlement.

## Key claims

- Authority-Inference Separation (AIS) treats a financial action intent as the control object.
- An independent, deterministic control plane must validate an agent-proposed action before it receives executable authority.
- Blockchain enforces the granted authority and records settlement evidence.
- Legal, service-delivery, accounting and human accountability obligations stay off-chain.
- Across 36 synthetic authorisation attacks, AIS blocked all, a direct-agent baseline accepted all 36, and a prompt-policy baseline accepted 20.
- Public ledgers can evidence settlement but cannot establish institutional mandate or legal accountability.
