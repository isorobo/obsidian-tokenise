# A prelude to the theory of Real-World Asset (RWA) Tokenization

## Citation

Wenpin Tang. "A prelude to the theory of Real-World Asset (RWA) Tokenization". arXiv (econ.GN), 14 September 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15797.

## One-line summary

A theory paper that classifies RWA tokenisation by the role of the underlying asset and builds two models, one on security design under asymmetric information and one on Digital Asset Treasuries.

## Key claims

- The paper introduces RWA tokenisation and links the market to classical financial theory through two economic models.
- It classifies tokenisation by the role of the underlying asset: standard RWA tokenisation, stablecoins and Digital Asset Treasuries (DATs).
- The first model treats tokenisation as security design under asymmetric information.
- Continuous secondary-market trading recovers information, which lowers adverse-selection costs over time.
- That effect changes both the issuance price and the optimal tokenisation design.
- The second model is a dynamic corporate-finance framework for DATs, where market valuation, capital-market financing and crypto accumulation interact, illustrated with Strategy as a case study.

## Significance

- It supplies a theoretical frame for a literature that is mostly empirical or taxonomic in the vault.
- It places stablecoins inside the RWA tokenisation family, which helps reconcile stablecoin and tokenised-asset sources.
- The secondary-market liquidity mechanism connects to the empirical liquidity evidence on tokenised RWAs.
