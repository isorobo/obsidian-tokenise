# Financial Fragility in Societies of LLM Agents

## Citation

Zhenhao Fu, Ruipeng Xu and Qibing Ren. "Financial Fragility in Societies of LLM Agents". arXiv (cs.AI; q-fin.GN), 25 September 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30940.

## One-line summary

Experiments with the FRAIL framework show that societies of individually capable LLM agents often produce bank runs and debt-rollover failures, and that early broad commitment is the key stabiliser.

## Key claims

- Individually rational LLM agents can collectively create unstable financial systems.
- The FRAIL framework tests agents in three scenarios: bank runs, debt rollover and reward crowdfunding.
- Fragility appeared across seven leading LLM models without any instruction to destabilise.
- Three stabilisation mechanisms were tested: compensated commitments, centralised agreements and participant-led coalitions.
- All three improved outcomes, and early broad commitment was the common success pattern.
- System-level design and evaluation are needed for AI financial safety.

## Significance

- It supplies evidence on collective risk from agentic AI in finance, relevant to run dynamics on stablecoins and tokenised funds.
- It supports the argument that agent governance must work at system level, not only per agent.
