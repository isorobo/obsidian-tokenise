# Evolution of Market Microstructure in the Age of AI

## Citation

Irene Aldridge. "Evolution of Market Microstructure in the Age of AI". arXiv, 27 September 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33058.

## One-line summary

A survey traces market design from floor trading to AI agents and argues that allocation is the missing layer in agent-commerce protocol stacks.

## Key claims

- The survey covers floor trading, electronic limit order books, high-frequency trading, batch auctions, dark pools, automated market makers and AI agents.
- Each technological shift moved the binding constraint of market design, from trader rationality to speed, to control of transaction ordering, to the dimensionality of what participants can report.
- It reviews evidence on high-frequency trading, trading venues, automated market makers, extractable value and algorithmic collusion.
- It centres on whether a market can allocate scarce goods efficiently and fairly without participants revealing everything they know and want.
- It identifies allocation as the missing layer of the agent-commerce protocol stack.
