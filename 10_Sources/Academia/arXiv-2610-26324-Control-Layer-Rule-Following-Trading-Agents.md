# The Control Layer Behind Rule-Following Agents Trading Across Market Conditions

## Citation

Naja, Saad; Odtallah, Nasser; Van-Praag, Jake; May, Sarah. "The Control Layer Behind Rule-Following Agents Trading Across Market Conditions". arXiv, 19 October 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26324.

## One-line summary

An empirical audit of four AI trading agents finds high adherence to declared strategies and risk rules across 2,627 closed positions, and offers a toolkit for separating rule-following from luck.

## Key claims

- The study analyses 2,627 closed positions from 12 February to 27 August 2026, mostly simulated against live prices with a 0.09% round-trip fee.
- About 18% of positions involved real Hyperliquid orders.
- The fleet of four agents finished net positive after fees, while one agent was unprofitable yet still rule-adherent.
- Agents kept 97.5% of gross loss within declared stops, and 23 of 24 runs respected the five-position limit.
- The authors state that the results are trade-level, not account-level returns, and do not prove a lasting edge.
- The paper provides an evaluation toolkit for distinguishing rule-following from luck.

## Significance

- Supplies evidence on whether autonomous trading agents obey declared controls, a core question for supervision of agentic AI in finance.
- Focuses on a control layer outside the model, in line with the vault's governance work on agentic finance.
- Evidence is small-scale and largely simulated, so treat the findings as indicative only.
