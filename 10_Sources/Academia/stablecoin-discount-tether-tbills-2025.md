# The Stablecoin Discount: Evidence of Tether's U.S. Treasury Bill Market Share in Lowering Yields

## Citation

Ante, Lennart, Aman Saggu, and Ingo Fiedler. "The Stablecoin Discount: Evidence of Tether's U.S. Treasury Bill Market Share in Lowering Yields". arXiv q-fin.GN, 2025-05-18. https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12413.

## One-line summary

Threshold regression analysis shows that Tether's $98.5 billion T-bill portfolio -- representing 1.6% of outstanding U.S. Treasury bills -- suppresses short-term yields by up to 6.3% per percentage point of market share above a critical 0.973% threshold, generating approximately $15 billion in annual interest savings for the U.S. government.

## Key claims

- Tether held approximately $98.5 billion in U.S. Treasury bills as of Q1 2025, representing 1.6% of all outstanding bills.
- Baseline analysis finds a 1% increase in Tether's T-bill market share reduces 1-month yields by 3.8%.
- Threshold regression identifies a critical 0.973% market share level above which yield effects amplify: a 1% increase above this threshold reduces yields by 6.3%.
- Tether's T-bill demand generates approximately $15 billion in annual interest savings for the U.S. government.
- The findings establish a quantifiable monetary policy channel through which private stablecoin reserve management affects sovereign debt markets.
- Stablecoin reserve composition decisions by a single issuer can produce systemic effects on short-term government funding costs.

## Significance

- Provides the strongest empirical evidence in the current literature that stablecoin reserve policy has a measurable monetary policy spillover into sovereign debt markets.
- Directly relevant to the wiki's monetary-policy and reserve-requirements domains, and to regulatory debates about stablecoin reserve requirements (MiCA, U.S. STABLE Act).
- The threshold effect establishes that stablecoin systemic risk is non-linear, a critical finding for the wiki's market-structure analysis.
- Reinforces the wiki's treatment of Tether as a systemically significant financial institution in the stablecoins domain.
