# Resolution BCB 521 - Virtual asset operations and the foreign exchange market

## Citation

Banco Central do Brasil. "Resolution BCB 521". Resolucoes BCB, 10 November 2025. https://www.bcb.gov.br/estabilidadefinanceira/exibenormativo?tipo=Resolu%C3%A7%C3%A3o%20BCB&numero=521.

## One-line summary

Resolution BCB 521 brings stablecoin and other virtual asset purchases, sales and cross-border transfers under Brazil's foreign exchange rules, alongside Resolutions 519 and 520 that licence virtual asset service providers.

## Key claims

- The BCB published Resolutions 519, 520 and 521 on 10 November 2025 to implement the Virtual Assets Law (Law 14,478 of 2022), creating a licence category of virtual asset service providers (SPSAVs) for brokers, custodians and intermediaries.
- Resolution 521 treats buying, selling or exchanging fiat-pegged virtual assets (stablecoins), including international transfers and payments, as foreign exchange operations.
- Transactions with unlicensed foreign counterparties are capped at US$100,000 per transfer.
- Transfers to and from self-custody wallets are covered when a provider intermediates them; providers must identify the wallet owner and verify the origin and destination of assets.
- The rules took effect on 2 February 2026, with mandatory reporting for capital-market and cross-border operations from 4 May 2026.
- Coverage reports that the BCB will not accept stablecoins whose collateralisation is algorithmic.
- The BCB cited legal certainty, avoiding regulatory arbitrage and aligning crypto activity with balance-of-payments statistics.

## Significance

- Brazil is the largest crypto market in Latin America, and this resolution fixes the legal character of stablecoin flows there as foreign exchange rather than a separate payments category.
- Resolution 561 of April 2026 later removed crypto from eFX settlement, so the two rules must be read together.
- Brazilian practice supplies a counterpoint to the GENIUS Act model of treating stablecoins as payment instruments.
