# Resolution BCB 561 - Electronic foreign exchange providers and virtual assets

## Citation

Banco Central do Brasil. "Resolution BCB 561". Resolucoes BCB, 30 April 2026. https://www.bcb.gov.br/estabilidadefinanceira/exibenormativo?tipo=Resolu%C3%A7%C3%A3o%20BCB&numero=561.

## One-line summary

Resolution BCB 561 bars electronic foreign exchange (eFX) providers from using stablecoins or other crypto to settle with foreign counterparties from 1 October 2026, while licensed virtual asset service providers keep their separate route.

## Key claims

- The BCB published Resolution 561 on 30 April 2026, amending the eFX framework.
- Payments and receipts between an eFX provider and its foreign counterparty must occur through traditional foreign exchange operations or Brazilian real accounts held by non-residents.
- The prohibition takes effect on 1 October 2026.
- The measure does not ban crypto transfers outright; investors and firms can still buy, sell, custody and transfer crypto under Resolution 521.
- Licensed virtual asset service providers can still use stablecoins for cross-border payments under Resolution 521.
- Reports describe stricter know-your-customer rules and data retention of up to ten years.
- Coverage cites concern that stablecoin-settled cross-border flows outside central bank oversight create money laundering and tax compliance risks.

## Significance

- It shows a regulator separating stablecoins as a licensed VASP activity from stablecoins as eFX settlement infrastructure.
- It narrows the permissive reading of Resolution 521 for payment fintechs, with consequences for BRL-denominated stablecoin issuers.
- The BCB governor has said about 90% of domestic crypto flow is linked to stablecoins, so the rule targets the main use case.
