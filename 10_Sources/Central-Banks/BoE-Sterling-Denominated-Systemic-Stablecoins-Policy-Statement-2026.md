# Sterling-denominated systemic stablecoins: Policy statement and consultation on draft Code of Practice

## Citation

Bank of England. "Sterling-denominated systemic stablecoins: Policy statement and consultation on draft Code of Practice". Bank of England Policy Statement, 22 June 2026. https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/paper/2026/ps/sterling-denominated-systemic-stablecoins.

## One-line summary

The Bank of England sets out its final policy positions for regulating sterling systemic stablecoin issuers and consults on a draft Code of Practice to implement them, to be finalised by end-2026.

## Key claims

- The Bank and the FCA would jointly regulate a stablecoin once HM Treasury recognises it as systemic.
- The Bank revised its backing asset proposals, raising the cap on interest-bearing short-term UK government debt from 60% to 70% (per the Bank's news release of the same date), with at least 30% held as central bank deposits.
- A temporary issuance guardrail on aggregate issuance of each systemic stablecoin replaces the earlier proposed holding limits; the news release gives an initial level of 40 billion pounds.
- The statement clarifies policy on capital, safeguarding, coinholder remuneration, redemption and failure arrangements.
- It confirms a planned Central Bank Liquidity Facility as a backstop and issuers' ability to place funds on deposit with the central bank.
- Feedback on the draft Code closed on 22 September 2026, and the Bank intends to finalise the Code by end-2026, with further supporting material in 2027.

## Significance

- Primary regulatory issuance for the UK systemic stablecoin regime, the central UK source for the stablecoins and reserve-requirements strands.
- The shift from holding limits to an issuance guardrail is a key design choice to compare against the US GENIUS Act rules in the vault.
- Backing asset mix (gilts plus unremunerated central bank deposits) bears directly on the reserve and monetary-hierarchy MOCs.
