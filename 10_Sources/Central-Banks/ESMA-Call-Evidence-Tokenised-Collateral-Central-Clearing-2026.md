# ESMA seeks evidence on the use of tokenised collateral in central clearing

## Citation

European Securities and Markets Authority. "ESMA seeks evidence on the use of tokenised collateral in central clearing". ESMA News, 9 October 2026. https://www.esma.europa.eu/press-news/esma-news/esma-seeks-evidence-use-tokenised-collateral-central-clearing.

## One-line summary

ESMA opened a call for evidence (ESMA91-1505572268-4934, responses due 15 January 2027) on whether EU central counterparties could safely accept tokenised collateral.

## Key claims

- ESMA launched a Call for Evidence on the potential use of tokenised collateral by central counterparties (CCPs).
- The call asks whether, and under what conditions, EU CCPs could use tokenised collateral safely and effectively.
- It covers how tokenisation may affect the transfer, management, protection and use of collateral across its lifecycle.
- Models examined include digital twins of assets held in traditional infrastructures, assets issued directly on DLT, hybrid arrangements, and interaction with tokenised cash and other settlement assets.
- Key questions include whether CCPs could access, transfer and convert tokenised collateral into liquidity after a clearing member default, and how client protection, segregation and settlement finality would be ensured.
- Contributions are due by 15 January 2027; ESMA will assess feedback in Q1 2027 and then decide on any regulatory or supervisory convergence action within its remit.

## Excerpts

> "Tokenisation has the potential to make Europe's financial markets more efficient, integrated and innovative."
> ~ article body (Verena Ross, Chair of ESMA)

> "it is key that the fundamental safeguards applicable to CCP collateral remain unchanged."
> ~ article body (Klaus Löber, Chair of ESMA's CCP Supervisory Committee)

> "Collateral must be of high quality, legally enforceable, highly liquid, and easily operationally available"
> ~ article body (Klaus Löber)

## Significance

- First ESMA instrument in the vault aimed at tokenised collateral in clearing, extending its tokenisation work beyond MiCA into post-trade infrastructure.
- Signals that ESMA will keep existing CCP collateral safeguards and test tokenisation against them.
- The Q1 2027 assessment is a follow-up point for any supervisory convergence action.
- Complements the DLT Pilot Regime review, which covers trading and settlement rather than clearing.
