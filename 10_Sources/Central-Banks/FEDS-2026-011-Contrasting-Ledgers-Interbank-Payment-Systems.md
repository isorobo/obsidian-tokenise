# Contrasting Ledgers: Considerations for U.S. Dollar Interbank Payment Systems

## Citation

Leistra, Melissa. "Contrasting Ledgers: Considerations for U.S. Dollar Interbank Payment Systems". Finance and Economics Discussion Series 2026-011, Federal Reserve Board, February 2026. https://doi.org/10.17016/FEDS.2026.011.

## One-line summary

The paper sets out three defining features of US dollar interbank settlement and tests three shared-ledger proposals against them.

## Key claims

- Current US dollar interbank payment systems share three characteristics: distinct operational perimeters, settlement assets confined to the issuer's perimeter, and ledgers linked through mutual account holding.
- Canada and Denmark depart from this model. Both let their central bank money settle on ledgers the central bank does not operate.
- The Regulated Liability Network proposal would let central bank, commercial bank, and regulated nonbank money share one ledger, with a separate partition for each issuer.
- The BIS unified ledger concept extends this further. It would combine central bank money, commercial bank money, and other financial assets on one programmable platform.
- The IMF's cross-border (XC) platform concept would settle multicurrency trades using tokenised, escrowed central bank reserves on a centralised venue.
- Altering any of the three key characteristics raises tradeoffs around market choice, the reorganisation of trust relationships, and the management of opposing commercial interests.
- The paper takes no position on any proposal. It sets out questions stakeholders should ask before altering the current settlement architecture.

## Excerpts

> "This paper describes the current U.S. dollar interbank payments landscape and identifies its key characteristics. It then discusses major considerations and potential tradeoffs that various conceptual alternatives might raise."
> ~ Abstract

> "Notwithstanding this variety, USD interbank systems have three key characteristics in common: operational perimeters of individual entities are distinct; settlement assets remain constrained within operational perimeters of their issuers; and ledgers are connected through entities holding accounts with one another."
> ~ Introduction (p. 2)

> "In November 2022, a group of financial institutions published a paper outlining a concept they titled, the Regulated Liability Network (RLN). The premise of an RLN was that utilization of new technologies should not require shifting the legal underpinnings and treatment of money."
> ~ Section 4, "RLN" (p. 20)

> "Settlement mechanics were more specifically described as being based on 'tokenized' central bank reserves. Participating central banks would establish accounts in which reserves could be 'escrowed' for the XC platform."
> ~ Section 4, "XC Platforms" (p. 26)

> "At times, an undercurrent seems to run within these proposals, that operational efficiency and hard-coded behavior constraints could also eliminate the financial risks that pass between payment or trading counterparties."
> ~ Section 5, "How are opposing interests managed?" (p. 30)

## Significance

- Documents, in one Fed staff paper, how RLN, BIS unified ledgers, and IMF XC platforms would each alter US dollar settlement architecture. This gives the wiki a primary-source comparison of the leading shared-ledger proposals.
- Names tokenised central bank reserves as the settlement mechanic of the IMF XC platform concept. This links wholesale CBDC design to cross-border tokenisation debates.
- Signals Federal Reserve staff scepticism toward claims that programmable, shared-ledger settlement eliminates counterparty risk rather than relocating it.
- Supplies the blockchain-settlement domain with an authoritative taxonomy (operational perimeter, settlement-asset containment, and account-linkage) against which any tokenised settlement proposal can be tested.
