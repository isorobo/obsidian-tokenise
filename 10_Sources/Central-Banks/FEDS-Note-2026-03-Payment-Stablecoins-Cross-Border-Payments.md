# Payment Stablecoins and Cross Border Payments: Benefits and Implications for Monetary Policy Implementation

## Citation

Kim, Kyungmin, Romina Ruprecht, and Mary-Frances Styczynski. "Payment Stablecoins and Cross Border Payments: Benefits and Implications for Monetary Policy Implementation". FEDS Notes, Federal Reserve Board, 30 March 2026. https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/payment-stablecoins-and-cross-border-payments-benefits-and-implications-for-monetary-policy-20260330.html.

## One-line summary

Payment stablecoins could reduce cross-border payment frictions by bypassing correspondent banking chains, but widespread adoption would require the Federal Reserve to recalibrate reserve management policies.

## Key claims

- Over 60% of wholesale cross-border payments route through one or more correspondent bank intermediaries, imposing costs and delays on end-users.
- The correspondent banking network contracted approximately 30% over the past decade, concentrating bottlenecks and raising costs for small banks and individuals.
- Payment stablecoins could allow parties to bypass correspondent chains, enabling direct settlement and reducing transaction fees.
- The monetary policy implications depend on which assets back the stablecoins: bank deposits, Treasury bills, or Federal Reserve reserves each alter demand for those assets differently.
- Widespread stablecoin adoption would require "recalibration" of the Federal Reserve's reserve management framework.
- The analysis assumes minimal access costs and favourable regulatory treatment, which may not materialise in practice.

## Significance

- Provides the Federal Reserve's first systematic analysis of payment stablecoin effects on cross-border payments infrastructure, directly relevant to the wiki's cross-border-payments domain.
- The three backing-asset scenarios — deposits, Treasuries, or Fed reserves — map directly to the policy debates around the GENIUS Act reserve requirements.
- Links stablecoin adoption to Federal Reserve balance sheet management, bridging the stablecoins and monetary-policy domains.
- Establishes the empirical baseline for correspondent banking contraction that motivates stablecoin adoption as a structural response.
