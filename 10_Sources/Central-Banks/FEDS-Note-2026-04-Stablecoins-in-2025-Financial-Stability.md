# Stablecoins in 2025: Developments and Financial Stability Implications

## Citation

Carapella, Francesca, Arazi Lubis, and Alexandros Vardoulakis. "Stablecoins in 2025: Developments and Financial Stability Implications". FEDS Notes, Federal Reserve Board, 8 April 2026. https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/stablecoins-in-2025-developments-and-financial-stability-implications-20260408.html.

## One-line summary

The stablecoin market grew 50% in 2025 to $317 billion, but complex intermediation chains, vertical integration, and deepening links to traditional finance compounded three structural vulnerabilities.

## Key claims

- Stablecoin market capitalisation reached approximately $317 billion by April 2026, roughly 50% above its level at the start of 2025.
- Stablecoins with higher-quality liquid reserves, notably USDC, gained market share relative to alternatives maintaining lower reserve quality.
- The July 2025 GENIUS Act established a regulatory framework for payment stablecoins, prompting shifts in issuer composition and reserve practices.
- Complex intermediation chains involving multiple third-party providers create contagion risks and obscure stress sources during crises.
- Vertical integration — where exchanges operate blockchains and stablecoin issuers develop infrastructure — complicates risk assessment across the value chain.
- Expanding connections with traditional payment infrastructure amplify systemic footprint and potential destabilisation impact.

## Significance

- Provides the Federal Reserve's annual stock-take of the stablecoin market, establishing the empirical baseline against which regulatory impacts are measured.
- Identifies three structural vulnerabilities — complex chains, vertical integration, and TradFi integration — that inform the wiki's stablecoins and market-structure analysis.
- Documents the GENIUS Act's first-year effects on reserve quality and issuer behaviour, making it load-bearing for the law-regulation and stablecoins domains.
- Complements FEDS 2026-037's theoretical fragility model with a market-level empirical picture.
