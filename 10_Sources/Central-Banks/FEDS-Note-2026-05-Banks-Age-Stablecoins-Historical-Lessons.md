# Banks in the Age of Stablecoins: Lessons from Their Historical Responses to Financial Innovations

## Citation

Hempel, Sam, JP Perez-Sangimino, and Jessie Jiaxu Wang. "Banks in the Age of Stablecoins: Lessons from Their Historical Responses to Financial Innovations". FEDS Notes, Federal Reserve Board, 1 May 2026. https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/banks-in-the-age-of-stablecoins-lessons-from-their-historical-responses-to-financial-innovations-20260501.html.

## One-line summary

Historical episodes — money market funds in the 1970s and PayPal in the 1990s — show that banks adapt to disintermediation threats through regulatory advocacy, product innovation, and strategic partnership rather than displacement.

## Key claims

- Banks have historically responded to competitive threats through regulatory engagement, product development, and partnership rather than through displacement.
- Money market funds grew from negligible to $220 billion by 1982, equivalent to 15% of bank deposits, but banks responded with NOW accounts, Money Market Deposit Accounts, and regulatory relief via phase-out of Regulation Q.
- Banks eventually competed with PayPal through mobile banking modernisation and industry consortia such as Zelle, which now processes over $1.5 trillion annually.
- Stablecoins combine characteristics of both prior innovations: balance-holding like MMFs and digital payment functionality like PayPal.
- Approximately half of survey respondent banks report prioritising growth in at least one stablecoin or digital asset-related area.
- Whether demand is served by stablecoins, tokenised deposits, or other instruments, banks' advantages — deposit insurance, regulatory trust, and integrated financial services — position them to remain central.

## Significance

- Establishes a historical counterfactual to displacement narratives, grounding the wiki's stablecoins analysis in banking sector adaptation evidence.
- The MMF and PayPal case studies map directly to the tokenised-deposit competitive dynamic, making the note load-bearing for the tokenised-deposits domain.
- The survey finding — half of banks pursuing stablecoin or digital asset growth — provides a current baseline for bank adoption of tokenisation-adjacent products.
- Complements the structural fragility analysis in FEDS 2026-037 with a competitive dynamics perspective.
