# Fifth Conference on the International Roles of the U.S. Dollar: Stablecoins, Digital Payments, and the International Role of the U.S. Dollar

## Citation

Correa, Ricardo, Linda Goldberg, Ritt Keerati, Juan M. Londono, and Fabiola Ravazzolo. "Fifth Conference on the International Roles of the U.S. Dollar: Stablecoins, Digital Payments, and the International Role of the U.S. Dollar". FEDS Notes, Federal Reserve Board, 16 July 2026. https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/fifth-conference-on-the-international-roles-of-the-u-s-dollar-stablecoins-digital-payments-and-the-ir-of-the-usd-20260716.html.

## One-line summary

Federal Reserve staff summarise a June 2026 conference finding that dollar-denominated stablecoins are broadening access to dollar finance and opening new shock-transmission channels, without displacing the dollar's international dominance.

## Key claims

- On 22 and 23 June 2026, the Federal Reserve Board and the New York Fed jointly hosted the fifth conference on the international roles of the US dollar.
- This year's conference centred on financial innovation, particularly digital assets and stablecoins, and their effect on how households, businesses, and intermediaries access dollar-denominated assets.
- Dollar-denominated stablecoins increasingly function as tokenised money, with transaction volumes now exceeding those of Bitcoin.
- Stablecoin-based payment rails can lower certain cross-border transaction costs, complementing rather than replacing correspondent banking.
- Research presented at the conference found that stablecoin demand shocks affect Treasury yields, exchange rates, and equity prices, creating new transmission channels for financial shocks.
- Traditional drivers of dollar demand, including US economic size, market depth, and institutional confidence, remain the dominant explanation for the dollar's continued international role.
- Governor Waller and Professor Prasad both argued that stablecoin growth is more likely to reinforce than erode dollar dominance, given the dollar-denomination of leading stablecoins and the absence of credible alternatives.

## Significance

- Records the Federal Reserve's own institutional view that stablecoins now function as tokenised dollar money at a scale exceeding Bitcoin transaction volumes.
- Links stablecoin growth directly to Treasury market and exchange rate transmission channels, extending the analysis in the March 2026 FEDS Note on payment stablecoins and cross-border payments.
- Signals that senior Fed leadership treats stablecoins as reinforcing, not threatening, dollar hegemony, a load-bearing policy framing for the wiki's monetary-policy domain.
- Supplies conference-level corroboration, alongside IMF-presented research, that stablecoin demand shocks are a recognised macro-financial transmission mechanism.
