# A Decade of U.S. Cross-Border Payments Efforts

## Citation

Elena Falcettoni. "A Decade of U.S. Cross-Border Payments Efforts". FEDS Notes, 26 August 2026. https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/a-decade-of-u-s-cross-border-payments-efforts-20260826.html.

## One-line summary

A Federal Reserve Board staff note reviews ten years of U.S. cross-border payments work and treats stablecoins and tokenisation as emerging channels that may change speed and cost.

## Key claims

- The U.S. accounts for about 16.6 percent of worldwide remittances sent, over USD 98 billion in 2023.
- In 2025, 73.1 percent of wholesale cross-border payments to North America were received within one hour.
- The Fedwire Funds Service migrated to ISO 20022 messaging in July 2025.
- The FedNow Service began operating in July 2023 with 24/7/365 availability, initially for domestic payments only.
- The note says the GENIUS Act framework will likely mean U.S. stablecoins are backed by safe and liquid assets, making them a viable alternative in payments.
- The "stablecoin sandwich" model converts local currency into dollar stablecoins, transfers them, then converts to the destination currency, and the note expects faster and cheaper transfers, especially for remittances.

## Significance

- It gives the Fed staff view of stablecoins as a cross-border payments option inside a wider review of existing infrastructure (Fedwire, FedNow).
- The stablecoin and tokenisation content is a section of a broader payments note, so treat it as context rather than a core tokenisation study.
- It complements the earlier Fed note on payment stablecoins and monetary policy implementation.
