# New Forms of Money and the U.S. Monetary Aggregates

## Citation

Kristen Payne and Mary-Frances Styczynski. "New Forms of Money and the U.S. Monetary Aggregates". FEDS Notes, 4 September 2026. https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/new-forms-of-money-and-the-u-s-monetary-aggregates-20260904.html.

## One-line summary

A Federal Reserve Board staff note sets out how tokenised deposits, tokenised money market funds and payment stablecoins could be classified and measured within the U.S. monetary aggregates.

## Key claims

- The note evaluates tokenised deposits, tokenised money market funds and payment stablecoins against a functional test (medium of exchange versus store of value) and practical measurement considerations.
- Tokenised deposits allow instant 24/7/365 payments on private blockchain networks while remaining backed by bank assets and covered by deposit insurance.
- Tokenised deposits are currently commingled with traditional deposits in reported figures, which are drawn from weekly and quarterly data sources.
- Tokenised money market funds hold the same low-risk assets as conventional money funds and are regulated by the SEC under Rule 2a-7.
- The GENIUS Act, passed in July 2025, created the first comprehensive federal framework for payment stablecoins in the United States.
- The GENIUS Act requires authorised issuers to report monthly on reserve assets, which gives a possible data source for measuring stablecoins.

## Significance

- It is a primary Fed staff treatment of how tokenised money instruments would enter the official money supply statistics (H.6 release).
- It extends the channel's earlier stablecoin and singleness-of-money material from financial stability to statistical measurement.
- It links tokenised deposits, tokenised funds and stablecoins in one framework, which helps compare the three instrument classes in the vault.
