# Stablecoins and (Non)Crypto Shocks: A 2026 Update

## Citation

Anadu, Azar, Baker, Cipriani, Eisenbach, Landoni, La Spada, Macchiavelli and Wang. "Stablecoins and (Non)Crypto Shocks: A 2026 Update". Liberty Street Economics, Federal Reserve Bank of New York, 31 July 2026. https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/07/stablecoins-and-noncrypto-shocks-a-2026-update/.

## One-line summary

NY Fed economists show that the 2023 SVB failure moved USDC's reserve risk from interest-rate risk to counterparty risk, evidence of deepening ties between stablecoin issuers and traditional finance.

## Key claims

- US dollar stablecoin market capitalisation rose by about $71 billion (30%) since April 2025, to about $308 billion, and the authors link the growth to the GENIUS Act of July 2025.
- Tether (USDT) and USDC hold over 80% of industry assets, but USDC reserves are mainly cash and short-term US government securities while USDT reserves include corporate bonds, gold, Bitcoin and secured loans.
- SVB held about 8% of USDC's reserves when it failed in 2023, which the authors use as a case study of a non-crypto shock.
- The Circle Reserve Fund (about 86% of USDC reserves as of March 2026) shortened its weighted average maturity, now below the 5th percentile of Treasury-only money market funds.
- The fund's repo holdings rose from zero to over 90% of net assets and now stand at 69%; FICC-sponsored repo reached 77% of repo holdings by Q4 2025, often with hedge-fund-type end counterparties.
- USDC's bank deposits became concentrated in global systemically important banks.
