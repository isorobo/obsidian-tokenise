# Minutes of the Meeting of the Treasury Borrowing Advisory Committee, April 29, 2025

## Citation

U.S. Department of the Treasury. "Minutes of the Meeting of the Treasury Borrowing Advisory Committee, April 29, 2025". Treasury Borrowing Advisory Committee Minutes, 30 April 2025. https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0122.

## One-line summary

The TBAC took up stablecoins as a formal committee charge and recorded dealer views that stablecoin growth, and likely legislation, could make digital assets a material source of Treasury bill demand.

## Key claims

- The meeting included a committee charge titled "Stablecoins", set under the heading "Committee Charge - April 29, 2025".
- The charge describes growth of the cryptocurrency and digital asset economy and the expansion of the stablecoin market, and notes that some stablecoins are moving toward paying interest.
- The charge records that money market funds are exploring tokenisation and that Congress is considering a definition for dollar-backed payment stablecoins.
- It asks whether tokenised money funds pose a risk if they compete with other payment or settlement instruments.
- Primary dealers said stablecoin providers already hold significant Treasury positions and may be required to hold Treasury bills under proposed legislation.
- Dealers agreed the digital asset space should be monitored as a potential source of Treasury demand.
- The discussion covered the effects of interest-bearing stablecoins on Treasury demand, dollar standing and insured depository institutions, including whether stablecoins create net new Treasury demand or reallocate it from banks and money market funds.

## Significance

- It is the earliest primary Treasury record in the vault that links stablecoin growth to T-bill demand, which is the channel that ties stablecoin reserves to sovereign debt markets.
- It frames the debate the GENIUS Act later settled on reserve composition, so it works as context for the rule-making sources.
- The tokenised money fund angle connects to the vault's treatment of tokenised money market funds as settlement instruments.
