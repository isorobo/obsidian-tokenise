# ESMA 2026 Annual Work Programme

## Citation

European Securities and Markets Authority. "ESMA 2026 Annual Work Programme." ESMA Publications, 3 October 2025. https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2025-10/ESMA22-50751485-1604_Annual_Work_Programme_2026.pdf.

## One-line summary

ESMA sets out its 2026 regulatory and supervisory priorities, with tokenisation monitoring, the DLT Pilot Regime review, and integrated MiCA market surveillance as explicit commitments.

## Key claims

- ESMA will monitor the impact of tokenisation on EU financial markets throughout 2026 and contribute to the European Commission's review of the DLT Pilot Regulation.
- ESMA will provide opinions on DLT market infrastructure authorisation applications and publish guidelines on exemptions granted to DLT market infrastructure operators.
- A second phase of ESMA's technical solution for EU-level integrated monitoring of crypto-asset markets under MiCA is scheduled for 2026 delivery.
- ESMA's 2026 work includes guidance to NCAs on prevention and detection of market abuse in crypto-asset trading, building on the July 2025 guidelines.
- The work programme signals that ESMA views artificial intelligence, distributed ledger technology, and decentralised finance as a combined risk cluster requiring coordinated attention.
- T+1 settlement cycle implementation and consolidated tape provider selection also feature as major 2026 infrastructure commitments.

## Significance

- The work programme is the authoritative forward signal for how EU securities regulation will develop through 2026, covering tokenisation, DLT, AI, and MiCA enforcement in one document.
- The commitment to monitor tokenisation impacts on EU financial markets establishes ESMA as a data-collecting body on RWA adoption, producing future empirical evidence for the wiki corpus.
- The DLT Pilot Regime review contribution positions ESMA as the lead technical adviser to the Commission on whether to make the regime permanent.
- The AI and DeFi cluster signals that ESMA intends to apply MiCA-style oversight concepts to agentic and automated trading, relevant to the wiki's agentic-AI coverage.
