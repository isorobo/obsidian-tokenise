# ESMA Launches Common Supervisory Action on CASPs' Digital Operational Resilience for Custody

## Citation

European Securities and Markets Authority (ESMA). "ESMA Launches Common Supervisory Action on CASPs' Digital Operational Resilience for Custody". ESMA, 8 July 2026. https://www.esma.europa.eu/press-news/esma-news/esma-launches-common-supervisory-action-casps-digital-operational-resilience.

## One-line summary

One week after the MiCA transitional period ends, ESMA launches a year-long coordinated review of how authorised crypto-asset service providers safeguard client assets in custody.

## Key claims

- ESMA and national competent authorities open a Common Supervisory Action examining the digital operational resilience of CASPs, with a specific emphasis on custody services.
- National competent authorities carry out risk-based assessments of authorised CASPs from mid-2026 through mid-2027.
- The review covers distributed ledger technology risks, including governance arrangements, key and storage management, transaction controls, incident detection and response, smart contract risks, and third-party dependencies.
- ESMA identifies digital operational resilience and CASPs as key areas of supervisory risk following the end of the MiCA transitional period on 1 July 2026.
- A consolidated final report goes to ESMA's Board of Supervisors in the second half of 2027.
- The review follows growth in the register of authorised CASPs, which reached 283 entities in the EU and EEA after the transitional deadline, with several large exchanges, including Binance, still unlicensed.

## Significance

- This is the first coordinated EU supervisory exercise on crypto-asset custody conducted after the MiCA transitional period closed, marking the shift from authorisation to active supervision.
- The named risk areas (key management, smart contract risk, third-party dependency) give the vault a concrete regulatory taxonomy for custody risk in tokenised-asset infrastructure.
- The timing, one week after the 1 July 2026 deadline, confirms ESMA treats custody failure as the primary systemic risk in the newly authorised CASP population, directly relevant to the vault's custody and intermediation doctrine coverage.
- Complements the vault's existing MiCA transitional-period sources by showing what supervisory action follows the wind-down phase.
