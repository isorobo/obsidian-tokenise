# Report on the Functioning and Review of the DLT Pilot Regime

## Citation

European Securities and Markets Authority. "Report on the Functioning and Review of the DLT Pilot Regime." ESMA Publications, 25 June 2025. https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2025-06/ESMA75-117376770-460_Report_on_the_functioning_and_review_of_the_DLTR_-_Art.14.pdf.

## One-line summary

ESMA's Article 14 review of the DLT Pilot Regime finds modest uptake but recommends making the regime permanent with expanded thresholds and eligible asset classes.

## Key claims

- Three DLT market infrastructures have received authorisation under the Pilot Regime: CSD Prague (October 2024), 21X AG (December 2024), and 360X AG (April 2025).
- Live trading activity across all three authorised infrastructures remains minimal since the regime entered force on 23 June 2022.
- Existing general thresholds are too restrictive to attract broad market participation and should be recalibrated to reflect the risk profile of individual business models.
- ESMA recommends the European Commission make the DLT Pilot Regime permanent and allow tiered, adjustable thresholds.
- ESMA further recommends extending eligible asset classes to include complex and illiquid instruments and removing activity-based thresholds for market infrastructures.
- Embedding DLT-specific provisions directly into existing sectoral frameworks (MiFID II, CSDR) would reduce regulatory overlap and improve legal certainty.

## Significance

- This report is the primary EU-level evaluation of the DLT Pilot Regime, directly shaping whether and how the bloc scales its tokenised securities infrastructure.
- ESMA's recommendation to expand eligible assets to complex and illiquid instruments opens the pathway for tokenised bonds, private credit, and real estate within the EU regulatory perimeter.
- The recommendation to embed DLT provisions into MiFID II and CSDR signals convergence of the crypto and traditional securities regulatory tracks, which is a key structural development for the wiki's law and finance coverage.
- The low authorisation uptake (three operators, minimal live trading) provides a baseline data point for measuring how EU tokenised markets mature through 2026 and beyond.
