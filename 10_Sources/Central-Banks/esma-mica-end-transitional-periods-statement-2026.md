# Statement on the End of Transitional Periods under MiCA

## Citation

European Securities and Markets Authority (ESMA). "Statement on the End of Transitional Periods under MiCA". ESMA, 17 April 2026. https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-04/ESMA75-113276571-1679_Statement_on_the_end_of_transitional_periods_under_MiCA.pdf.

## One-line summary

ESMA sets binding supervisory expectations for orderly wind-down by unauthorised CASPs and confirms no extension of the 1 July 2026 MiCA deadline, while specifying client migration and custody outsourcing restrictions.

## Key claims

- The MiCA transitional period expires across the EU on 1 July 2026. No extension will be granted.
- Unauthorised CASPs must cease onboarding new EU clients immediately and limit services to asset transfers, reallocation, and position closures.
- CASPs must have credible wind-down plans in place with advance notice to clients and pathways for asset transfer to authorised CASPs or self-hosted wallets.
- Custody and certain other MiCA services cannot be outsourced to non-EU or unauthorised third-country entities, requiring unwind of existing arrangements.
- ESMA co-ordinates NCAs through a supervisory forum to identify best practices and ensure consistent application across EEA jurisdictions.
- CASPs established outside the EU cannot provide MiCA services to EU clients, including in business-to-business contexts.

## Excerpts

> "After this date, any entity providing crypto-asset services to EU clients without a MiCA licence will be in breach of EU law and must cease offering such services."
> ~ ESMA75-113276571-1679, 17 April 2026

> "ESMA is promoting supervisory convergence between Member States in three ways: providing a forum where supervisors from the NCAs can exchange views on practical cases in their jurisdictions, as well as identifying best practices to promote convergence."
> ~ ESMA75-113276571-1679, 17 April 2026

> "Custody and certain other services cannot be outsourced to non-EU or unauthorised third-country entities meaning that any such arrangements must be unwound."
> ~ ESMA75-113276571-1679, 17 April 2026

## Significance

- This statement is the primary regulatory instrument confirming the hard 1 July 2026 deadline, removing legal uncertainty that had surrounded MiCA's phased transition across Member States.
- The custody outsourcing restriction has direct implications for international tokenisation arrangements where EU CASPs rely on non-EU custody infrastructure.
- The supervisory convergence forum mechanism establishes precedent for cross-border regulatory co-ordination that will inform how tokenised-asset markets develop under MiCA.
