# Guidelines on Supervisory Practices to Prevent and Detect Market Abuse under MiCA

## Citation

European Securities and Markets Authority. "Guidelines on Supervisory Practices to Prevent and Detect Market Abuse under MiCA." ESMA Publications, 9 July 2025. https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2025-07/ESMA75-453128700-1039_Guidelines_on_supervisory_practices_to_prevent_and_detect_market_abuse__MiCA_.pdf.

## One-line summary

ESMA sets binding supervisory standards for national competent authorities to detect and prevent insider dealing and market manipulation in crypto-asset markets across the EU.

## Key claims

- The guidelines apply to all national competent authorities (NCAs) responsible for MiCA supervision, requiring application within three months of official publication.
- NCAs must use a data-driven approach to surveillance, drawing on publicly available data, regulatory transaction data from CASPs, and on-chain data reconciliation where possible.
- Supervisory activity must be risk-based and proportionate, with NCAs required to develop dedicated staff expertise and technological tools specific to crypto markets.
- The guidelines address three forms of market abuse: insider dealing, unlawful disclosure of inside information, and market manipulation.
- Crypto trading's cross-border character and intensive use of social media require NCAs to develop supervisory cultures distinct from those used for traditional securities.
- NCAs must notify ESMA of their compliance status within two months of publication in all EU languages.

## Excerpts

> "Surveillance activity shall include publicly available data, regulatory data on orders and transactions obtained from CASPs and, where possible, the reconciliation of on-chain, off-chain, and cross-market data."
> ~ Section 3: Data and Surveillance Requirements

> "The guidelines set out general principles requiring supervisory activity to be risk-based and proportionate."
> ~ Section 2: General Principles

> "NCAs must have dedicated staff with the appropriate expertise and experience on the subject, the necessary technological tools and a data-driven approach to supervision."
> ~ Section 4: Resources and Expertise

## Significance

- These guidelines are the operative supervisory instrument for market abuse enforcement under MiCA, binding NCAs across all 27 EU member states from October 2025.
- The on-chain data reconciliation requirement signals that EU supervisors will use blockchain analytics as a primary tool, establishing a precedent for other jurisdictions.
- The cross-border and social-media provisions address features unique to crypto markets, extending the Market Abuse Regulation (MAR) framework into digital asset territory.
- For the wiki, this document establishes the EU's post-MiCA market integrity architecture alongside the DLT Pilot Regime review.
