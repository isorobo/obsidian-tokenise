# ESMA Interim MiCA Register, Post-Deadline Update

## Citation

European Securities and Markets Authority (ESMA). "Interim MiCA Register". ESMA, updated 3 July 2026. https://www.esma.europa.eu/esmas-activities/digital-finance-and-innovation/markets-crypto-assets-regulation-mica.

## One-line summary

ESMA's first register update after the 1 July 2026 MiCA deadline adds 37 crypto-asset service providers, including Standard Chartered's Luxembourg subsidiary, bringing the authorised total to 280.

## Key claims

- ESMA maintains a central register of crypto-asset white papers, authorised crypto-asset service providers, and non-compliant entities under Articles 109 and 110 of MiCA.
- The register updates weekly and is published as a set of five CSV files pending integration into ESMA's IT systems.
- The 3 July 2026 update raises the authorised CASP count from 243 to 280, the first major revision after the 1 July deadline.
- Standard Chartered received MiCA authorisation through its Luxembourg subsidiary on 29 June 2026, alongside an electronic money institution licence.
- Germany, France, and the Netherlands hold the largest shares of authorised CASPs by home state.
- Only about 17 to 20% of the more than 1,200 entities previously operating under national regimes had secured full MiCA authorisation by the deadline, leaving the remainder to wind down or continue unlawfully.

## Excerpts

> "ESMA will publish the latest version of the register on weekly intervals."
> ~ ESMA MiCA register page, 3 July 2026 update

## Significance

- The register is the authoritative, continuously updated record of which entities may lawfully provide crypto-asset services in the EU, making it a primary reference for the vault's licensing and market-structure coverage.
- The jump from 243 to 280 authorised CASPs in a single week quantifies the post-deadline authorisation surge that the vault's MiCA transitional-period sources anticipated.
- Standard Chartered's authorisation is the first clear signal of a global systemically important bank obtaining MiCA CASP status, relevant to the vault's coverage of commercial banks entering tokenised-asset infrastructure.
- The gap between the roughly 1,200 previously operating entities and the 280 authorised confirms the scale of market exit driven by MiCA, evidence for the vault's analysis of licensing as a market-structuring doctrine.
