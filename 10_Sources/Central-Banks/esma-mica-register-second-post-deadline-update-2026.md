# ESMA Interim MiCA Register, Second Post-Deadline Update

## Citation

European Securities and Markets Authority (ESMA). "Interim MiCA Register". ESMA, updated 16 July 2026. https://www.esma.europa.eu/esmas-activities/digital-finance-and-innovation/markets-crypto-assets-regulation-mica.

## One-line summary

ESMA's second weekly register update after the 1 July 2026 MiCA deadline adds 14 crypto-asset service providers, including Ripple Payments Europe, raising the authorised total to 294.

## Key claims

- The 16 July 2026 update raises the authorised CASP count from 280 to 294, adding 14 entities in a single week.
- Ripple Payments Europe, based in Luxembourg, and OSL EU GmbH, based in Austria, join the register among the new entrants.
- Traditional banks and cooperative banking networks dominate the new authorisations, including Portugal's Bison Bank, Croatia's state-owned Hrvatska poštanska banka, two German cooperative banks, and Liechtenstein-based Kaiser Partner Privatbank.
- ESMA republishes the register weekly as national competent authorities feed newly granted approvals into the interim system.
- Authorisations continued at pace after the 1 July 2026 deadline, with 51 CASPs added across the three weeks from 23 June to 16 July 2026, against expectations of market contraction.
- Withdrawn licences remain published on the register with the effective withdrawal date attached, preserving a historical record of exited entities.

## Excerpts

> "ESMA will publish the latest version of the register on weekly intervals."
> ~ ESMA MiCA register page, last update 16 July 2026

## Significance

- The continued weekly growth in authorised CASPs, from 243 before the deadline to 294 by 16 July 2026, shows the MiCA authorisation pipeline processing backlog applications rather than stalling after the transitional cut-off.
- Ripple's authorisation is a further signal of a major global crypto-native payments firm securing EU-wide market access under MiCA, relevant to the vault's coverage of stablecoin and payment-token infrastructure.
- The concentration of new entrants among traditional and cooperative banks confirms a pattern, first evidenced by Standard Chartered's authorisation, of regulated deposit-takers entering crypto-asset order execution rather than crypto-native firms alone.
- The register's practice of retaining withdrawn licences with effective dates gives the vault a verifiable dataset for tracking market exit alongside authorisation growth.
