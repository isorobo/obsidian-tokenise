# Financial Stability Implications of Generative AI: Taming the Animal Spirits

## Citation

Hansen, Anne Lundgaard, and Seung Jung Lee. "Financial Stability Implications of Generative AI: Taming the Animal Spirits". Finance and Economics Discussion Series (FEDS) 2025-090, Federal Reserve Board, September 2025. https://doi.org/10.17016/FEDS.2025.090.

## One-line summary

Laboratory experiments with large language model agents find that generative AI reduces herd-driven asset bubbles under private information but can be steered into stability-relevant herding when told to maximise profit.

## Key claims

- The paper tests how generative AI adoption affects financial stability by running large language models through laboratory-style replications of classic human herd-behaviour experiments.
- AI agents rely predominantly on private information rather than public market trends, producing more rational trading decisions than the human subjects in the original experiments.
- Wider use of AI-powered investment advice could reduce asset price bubbles that arise from herd-driven animal spirits.
- Explicitly instructing AI agents to maximise profit induces optimal herding, a distinct behaviour from bias-driven herding that still carries financial stability implications.
- AI agents are not purely algorithmic decision-makers. They inherit elements of human conditioning and bias under certain experimental variations.
- Optimal herding improves market discipline in the experiments but does not remove the systemic risk that correlated agent behaviour can pose.

## Significance

- Supplies primary Federal Reserve staff evidence on agentic-AI trading behaviour, distinct from the vault's arXiv-sourced agentic-trading literature, and grounds the topic in a central bank financial-stability lens rather than a pure computer-science benchmark.
- Establishes that instructable, profit-maximising AI agents can converge on correlated herding, a mechanism directly relevant to systemic risk assessment as agentic AI takes on trading and advisory roles.
- Complicates a simple "AI reduces bubbles" narrative by showing the effect is conditional on how agents are instructed, a finding load-bearing for any governance or supervisory framework aimed at agentic trading systems.
- Fills a gap in the fed-staff-papers channel, which had indexed extensive Fed work on stablecoins, tokenised deposits, and DLT settlement but no prior Fed staff paper on agentic-AI trading behaviour itself.
