# Banks in the Age of Stablecoins: Some Possible Implications for Deposits, Credit, and Financial Intermediation

## Citation

Wang, Jessie Jiaxu. "Banks in the Age of Stablecoins: Some Possible Implications for Deposits, Credit, and Financial Intermediation". FEDS Notes, Federal Reserve Board, 17 December 2025. https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/banks-in-the-age-of-stablecoins-implications-for-deposits-credit-and-financial-intermediation-20251217.html.

## One-line summary

Stablecoin expansion may shrink bank deposits by $190 to $408 billion and constrain lending, depending on whether issuers obtain Federal Reserve master account access and how banks adapt.

## Key claims

- Stablecoin adoption reduces bank deposits through three channels: direct displacement of deposits held as stablecoin reserves outside the banking system, reserve recycling through issuer bank accounts, and structural substitution of payment functions.
- Under moderate adoption scenarios, the analysis projects loan contractions of $190 to $408 billion, with the magnitude depending on issuer reserve management and whether issuers gain Federal Reserve master account access.
- If stablecoin issuers receive Fed master account access, reserve flows bypass commercial banks entirely, amplifying deposit outflows and their credit multiplier effects.
- Widespread stablecoin adoption could unbundle payment services from credit intermediation, altering the competitive landscape and potentially concentrating risk in systemically important issuers.
- Tokenised deposits, which preserve deposit status and Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation coverage, emerge as a structurally distinct alternative that retains banking system credit creation capacity.

## Significance

- Provides the first Federal Reserve staff quantification of deposit displacement under the GENIUS Act regulatory framework, load-bearing for the vault's monetary-policy and stablecoins domains.
- Distinguishes tokenised deposits from fiat-backed stablecoins as a structurally preferable design for preserving bank intermediation, directly relevant to the vault's tokenised-deposits domain.
- Fed master account access emerges as the critical policy variable determining whether stablecoin growth accelerates or moderates bank deposit contraction.
- Informs analysis of the reserve-requirements doctrine: how reserve management rules shape systemic credit supply is a core question for the vault's law-and-regulation corpus.
