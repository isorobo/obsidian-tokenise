# A Framework for Understanding the Vulnerabilities of New Money-Like Products

## Citation

Anadu, Kenechukwu, Patrick McCabe, JP Perez-Sangimino, and Nathan Swem. "A Framework for Understanding the Vulnerabilities of New Money-Like Products". Finance and Economics Discussion Series (FEDS) 2026-002, Federal Reserve Board, 6 January 2026. https://doi.org/10.17016/FEDS.2026.002.

## One-line summary

Tokenised money market funds, money market ETFs, and stablecoins share five structural vulnerability features with traditional money market funds, and regulators must assess each dimension as these products evolve.

## Key claims

- New money-like products — including tokenised money market funds (MMFs), money market ETFs, and fiat-backed stablecoins — can be transformative for finance but carry systemic vulnerability features analogous to traditional MMFs.
- The framework identifies five vulnerability dimensions: liquidity transformation, threshold effects, money-like asset status, contagion risks, and reactive investor behaviour.
- Tokenised MMFs exhibit heightened vulnerability on the threshold-effects dimension because blockchain-based redemption mechanics can accelerate first-mover advantage dynamics during stress.
- Stablecoins score highly on the money-like status and reactive-investor dimensions, making them susceptible to rapid run dynamics at a scale that can transmit to traditional financial markets.
- Vulnerability assessments must be dynamic: a product's risk profile will change as use cases expand, counterparty networks deepen, and regulatory frameworks mature.

## Significance

- Provides the first comprehensive Federal Reserve staff analytical framework for comparing stablecoins, tokenised MMFs, and money market ETFs as a unified asset class of money-like instruments.
- Establishes the theoretical basis for regulatory intervention thresholds: the five vulnerability dimensions give policymakers a structured basis for calibrating reserve and liquidity requirements.
- Directly relevant to the vault's stablecoins and tokenised-deposits domains, and to the reserve-requirements and intermediation doctrines.
- An earlier draft was presented at the Federal Reserve Banks of Boston and New York Second Conference on Stablecoins and Tokenization in May 2025, establishing this as a load-bearing document in Fed staff thinking.
