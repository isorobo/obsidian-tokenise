# Written Reply to Parliamentary Question on Agentic AI in Financial Services

## Citation

Monetary Authority of Singapore. "Written Reply to Parliamentary Question on Agentic AI in Financial Services". Singapore Parliament, 5 August 2026. https://www.mas.gov.sg/news/parliamentary-replies/2026/written-reply-to-parliamentary-question-on-agentic-ai-in-financial-services.

## One-line summary

MAS confirmed that its forthcoming binding AI Risk Management Guidelines apply to agentic AI, rejecting a purely voluntary path in favour of a principles-based supervisory backstop.

## Key claims

- Ms Mariam Jaafar, MP for Sembawang GRC, asked whether MAS would move from the industry-led SAFR framework to mandatory supervisory requirements, and on what timeline.
- Mr Gan Kim Yong, Deputy Prime Minister and Chairman of MAS, replied that MAS is taking a principles-based approach given AI's fast-evolving nature.
- MAS's proposed Guidelines on Artificial Intelligence Risk Management, consulted on in November 2025, apply to all AI use cases by financial institutions, including agentic AI.
- The Guidelines set supervisory expectations for board and senior management oversight, risk management frameworks, and AI life cycle controls; MAS said they will be finalised soon.
- MAS pointed to the SAFR white paper and the Project Mindforge AI Risk Management Toolkit as industry-developed implementation resources sitting underneath the binding Guidelines.
- The reply gave no fixed implementation date for the finalised Guidelines beyond stating they are forthcoming.

## Excerpts

> "Given AI's fast-evolving nature, MAS is taking a principles-based approach to guide safe and responsible AI adoption."
> ~ Written Reply, MAS to Parliament, 5 August 2026 (as reported in secondary coverage)

## Significance

- The reply places agentic AI squarely inside MAS's binding supervisory perimeter, not merely inside the voluntary SAFR framework, closing a gap the wiki previously tracked as open.
- It confirms Singapore as an early mover in formally extending AI risk-management guidelines to autonomous financial agents, ahead of comparable US and EU frameworks.
- It clarifies the relationship between the binding AI Risk Management Guidelines and the industry-led SAFR framework and Project Mindforge toolkit: the latter are implementation resources under the former's supervisory umbrella.
- The reply is a direct parliamentary accountability record for MAS's agentic-AI regulatory stance, useful for tracking the transition from consultation (November 2025) to finalised guidance.
