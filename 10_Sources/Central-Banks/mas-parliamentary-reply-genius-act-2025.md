# Written Reply to Parliamentary Question on Impact of US GENIUS Act on Singapore's Competitiveness for Regulated Digital Assets

## Citation

Monetary Authority of Singapore. "Written Reply to Parliamentary Question on Impact of US GENIUS Act on Singapore's Competitiveness for Regulated Digital Assets". Singapore Parliament, 24 September 2025. https://www.mas.gov.sg/news/parliamentary-replies/2025/written-reply-to-parliamentary-question-on-stablecoins.

## One-line summary

MAS assessed the US GENIUS Act's likely effects on Singapore's stablecoin hub position and confirmed it will seek recognition as a comparable regime while accelerating legislative amendments to its own stablecoin framework.

## Key claims

- The US Senate passed the GENIUS Act in July 2025, establishing a regulatory regime for payment stablecoins; rule-making precedes implementation by January 2027.
- Market participants expect the GENIUS Act to spur global interest in stablecoins, but the scale and pace of adoption remain uncertain.
- MAS's 2023 stablecoin regulatory framework covers single-currency stablecoins pegged to the Singapore dollar or any G10 currency; MAS will seek US recognition as a comparable regime.
- MAS is preparing legislative amendments to formalise its stablecoin regime as primary legislation and will issue a public consultation.
- Singapore-based financial institutions have tested stablecoins for programmable rewards, conditional payments, and cross-border payment settlement, building a domestic use-case base before US adoption accelerates.

## Excerpts

> "MAS will seek US recognition of Singapore's Stablecoin Regulatory Framework as a comparable regime under the GENIUS Act."
> ~ Written Reply, MAS to Parliament, 24 September 2025 (as reported in secondary coverage)

## Significance

- The reply is the primary public record of MAS's strategy for positioning Singapore within the post-GENIUS Act global stablecoin order.
- Seeking comparable-regime recognition under US law is a novel regulatory manoeuvre that, if successful, would give MAS-regulated stablecoins access to the US payment ecosystem.
- The announced legislative amendments will elevate stablecoin regulation from administrative guidance to statute, affecting licensing, reserve requirements, and enforcement powers.
- The response confirms MAS views its regulatory framework as internationally competitive rather than merely domestic, supporting cross-border stablecoin use for wholesale settlement.
