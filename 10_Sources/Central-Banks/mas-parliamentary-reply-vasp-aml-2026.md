# Written Reply to Parliamentary Question on Virtual Asset Service Providers

## Citation

Monetary Authority of Singapore. "Written Reply to Parliamentary Question on Virtual Asset Service Providers". Singapore Parliament, 5 August 2026. https://www.mas.gov.sg/news/parliamentary-replies/2026/written-reply-to-parliamentary-question-on-virtual-asset-service-providers.

## One-line summary

MAS confirmed 37 licensed digital payment token service providers out of close to 300 applicants, and defended Singapore's licensing regime against money-laundering risk.

## Key claims

- Ms He Ting Ru, MP for Sengkang GRC, asked about money-laundering and proliferation-financing risk from Singapore's role as a major virtual-asset-services hub.
- Mr Gan Kim Yong, Deputy Prime Minister and Chairman of MAS, said Singapore was one of the first countries to bring digital payment token service providers (DPTSPs) under financial sector regulation.
- MAS applies stringent licensing criteria; of close to 300 applications, only 37 DPTSPs currently hold a licence.
- MAS maintains close post-licensing supervision and has taken regulatory action against DPTSPs that fail to meet standards.
- MAS conducts sector surveillance for emerging money-laundering, terrorism-financing, and proliferation-financing threats, and engages DPTSPs proactively on risk awareness.
- Singapore participates in the Financial Action Task Force's work to drive consistent global implementation of its standards; the FATF's most recent assessment recognised the strength of Singapore's AML/CFT framework.

## Excerpts

> "Singapore was one of the first countries in the world to bring DPTSPs under financial sector regulation, recognising the inherently higher risks posed by their activities."
> ~ Written Reply, MAS to Parliament, 5 August 2026

> "MAS applies stringent licensing criteria, and admits only players who demonstrate ability to put in place systems and processes to mitigate the relevant risks, and comply with MAS' regulations."
> ~ Written Reply, MAS to Parliament, 5 August 2026

> "In its recent assessment of Singapore's AML/CFT framework earlier this year, the FATF recognised the strength of Singapore's system and MAS' effectiveness in regulating the DPTSPs."
> ~ Written Reply, MAS to Parliament, 5 August 2026

## Significance

- The 37-of-300 licensing ratio is a concrete, citable data point on the stringency of Singapore's digital-asset gatekeeping regime, relevant wherever the wiki compares jurisdictional licensing thresholds.
- The reply is the primary public record of MAS's AML/CFT posture toward DPTSPs, the licensing class that regulated stablecoin issuers and tokenisation platforms sit within.
- FATF's endorsement of Singapore's framework strengthens the credibility of MAS-licensed venues as counterparties for tokenised-asset and stablecoin settlement infrastructure tracked elsewhere in the wiki.
- The reply gives law-doctrine grounding (licensing, aml-cft) for sources that otherwise treat Singapore's digital-asset regime only from the tokenisation or stablecoin angle.
