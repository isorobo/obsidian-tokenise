# Brazil Makes Stablecoin Changes, Limiting Benefits. May Keep BRL Onshore

## Citation

Ledger Insights. "Brazil Makes Stablecoin Changes, Limiting Benefits. May Keep BRL Onshore". Ledger Insights, 6 July 2026. https://www.ledgerinsights.com/brazil-makes-stablecoin-changes-limiting-benefits-may-keep-brl-onshore/.

## One-line summary

Banco Central do Brasil has raised stablecoin transaction costs and proposed transfer delays, constraining stablecoin use for cross-border payments to protect monetary sovereignty.

## Key claims

- Banco Central do Brasil made a series of regulatory changes and legislative proposals over recent months that together significantly constrain stablecoin benefits for cross-border payments.
- The central bank increased stablecoin transaction costs from around 0.1% to as much as 1% by barring funds from acting as liquidity providers, treating that activity as foreign exchange intermediation.
- The bank separately proposed expedited fraud rulemaking imposing delays of up to 24 hours on stablecoin transfers exceeding $10,000 to offshore destinations or self-hosted wallets.
- Virtual asset service providers can implement procedures to expedite release of delayed funds, but the proposal has drawn significant industry pushback.
- The combined measures aim to protect monetary sovereignty and the integrity of Brazil's foreign exchange market by discouraging use of stablecoins as a Brazilian real substitute.

## Significance

- Provides a Latin American case study of a central bank actively narrowing stablecoin cross-border payment advantages to defend monetary sovereignty and FX market integrity.
- Illustrates a regulatory technique, reclassifying stablecoin liquidity provision as FX intermediation, that other emerging-market central banks may replicate.
- The proposed transfer-delay rule for large offshore and self-custodied transfers is relevant to AML/CFT doctrine on stablecoin payment rails.
- Broadens the wiki's jurisdictional coverage of stablecoin regulation beyond the US, EU and Asia-Pacific into South America.
