# CFTC Collateral Rule Change Could Boost Tokenized MMFs

## Citation

Ledger Insights. "CFTC Collateral Rule Change Could Boost Tokenized MMFs". Ledger Insights, 17 July 2026. https://www.ledgerinsights.com/cftc-collateral-rule-change-could-boost-tokenized-mmf/.

## One-line summary

The CFTC finalised a rule expanding which money market funds qualify as initial margin for uncleared swaps, removing a restriction that excluded funds using repo and securities lending.

## Key claims

- The CFTC finalised a rule change expanding eligible money market fund collateral for initial margin on uncleared swaps.
- The rule removes a prior restriction that excluded money market funds using reverse repo, repo and securities lending from qualifying as margin.
- Government money market funds regulated under SEC Rule 2a-7 participate in around $1.7 trillion of Treasury repo transactions as of October 2025.
- The change applies primarily to interest rate and foreign exchange swaps, the dominant categories of over-the-counter derivatives activity.
- The CFTC rejected industry proposals to cap repo volumes, impose additional haircuts, or mandate central clearing of repo within qualifying funds.
- Prime money market funds remain ineligible as swap margin collateral under the finalised rule.

## Excerpts

> "Reverse repo is considered to be relatively low risk. If the cash borrower goes bankrupt, the MMF keeps hold of the government securities."
> ~ Ledger Insights article body

## Significance

- Widens the pool of money market funds, including tokenised money market fund products, eligible as swap margin, a concrete collateral-use channel for tokenised instruments.
- Bears on secured-transactions doctrine by clarifying which fund structures a swap counterparty may rely on for initial margin without additional haircuts.
- Complements the DTCC's live tokenised-securities collateral trials by widening the regulatory eligibility of the underlying fund instrument class.
- Relevant to tracking how US derivatives market infrastructure absorbs tokenised money market funds as working collateral through 2026.
