# ECB's Cipollone Argues Digital Euro Will Shield Banks from Stablecoin Encroachment

## Citation

Ledger Insights. "ECB's Cipollone Argues Digital Euro Will Shield Banks from Stablecoin Encroachment". Ledger Insights, 20 July 2026. https://www.ledgerinsights.com/ecbs-cipollone-argues-digital-euro-will-shield-banks-from-stablecoin-encroachment/.

## One-line summary

ECB Executive Board member Piero Cipollone told Federcasse that a digital euro would protect banks from stablecoin and mobile-payment encroachment on deposits and fee income.

## Key claims

- Piero Cipollone delivered remarks at Federcasse's annual meeting on the digital euro's role against stablecoins.
- Cipollone argued private stablecoins and mobile payment systems reduce banks' transaction visibility and fee income.
- He stated public money serves no single private interest and cannot be used to impose terms on citizens who hold it.
- Cipollone argued a euro must be a euro wherever spent, guaranteed by an institution acting in the public interest.
- The digital euro's design excludes interest payments and imposes holding limits, intended to limit deposit flight.
- Cipollone framed these design features as preserving banks' credit-assessment role and fee structures against stablecoin erosion.

## Excerpts

> "Public money does not serve any single private interest, and no-one can use it to impose terms in their favour on the citizens that hold it."
> ~ Piero Cipollone, ECB Executive Board member

> "A euro is a euro wherever it is spent, and it is guaranteed by an institution whose actions are designed to safeguard the public interest."
> ~ Piero Cipollone, ECB Executive Board member

## Significance

- States the ECB's clearest public rationale for the digital euro as a defensive response to stablecoin growth, not only a payments-efficiency project.
- Confirms holding limits and no-interest design as deliberate anti-disintermediation tools rather than incidental features.
- Positions the digital euro directly against dollar-denominated stablecoins in the EU retail payments market.
- Relevant to comparing the ECB's CBDC strategy against the US GENIUS Act's bank-preserving stablecoin licensing model.
