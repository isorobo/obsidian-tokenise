# EU Parliament Backs Stablecoin Multi-Issuance, Dismissing Ban Proposal

## Citation

Ledger Insights. "EU Parliament Backs Stablecoin Multi-Issuance, Dismissing Ban Proposal". Ledger Insights, 9 July 2026. https://www.ledgerinsights.com/eu-parliament-backs-stablecoin-multi-issuance-dismissing-ban-proposal/.

## One-line summary

The European Parliament has adopted a non-binding digital assets report endorsing regulated stablecoin multi-issuance, rejecting the European Systemic Risk Board's recommendation to ban the practice under MiCA.

## Key claims

- The European Parliament adopted a digital assets report by 390 votes to 86, its clearest statement yet on stablecoin multi-issuance under MiCA.
- Parliament wants multi-issuance placed on a sound legal footing with safeguards, contrary to the European Systemic Risk Board's earlier recommendation to prohibit the practice.
- Circle already operates a multi-issuance structure, issuing USDC in the United States and separately through its French MiCA-licensed entity, with tokens fully interchangeable and reserves allocated proportionally.
- France's ACPR asked the European Commission in 2024 to clarify whether MiCA permits multi-issuance for e-money tokens, and national regulators have since reached differing conclusions.
- The report is non-binding, but its wording typically resurfaces in binding legislation, and the Commission is reviewing MiCA until 30 September 2026, with a legislative proposal expected in 2027.

## Significance

- Signals a regulatory fault line inside the EU between Parliament and the ESRB over whether stablecoin multi-issuance threatens singleness of money, engaging doctrine on reserve requirements and licensing.
- Provides an early marker for the Commission's MiCA review, due to conclude September 2026, ahead of an expected 2027 legislative proposal.
- Clarifies the regulatory basis on which USDC and comparable multi-jurisdictional stablecoins currently operate in the EU market.
- Relevant to comparative analysis of US GENIUS Act foreign-issuer equivalence provisions against the EU's emerging multi-issuance stance.
