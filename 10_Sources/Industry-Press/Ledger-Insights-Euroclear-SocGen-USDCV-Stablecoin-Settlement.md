# Euroclear to Adopt SocGen USDCV Stablecoin for Commercial Paper Settlement

## Citation

Ledger Insights. "Euroclear to Adopt SocGen USDCV Stablecoin for Commercial Paper Settlement". Ledger Insights, 25 June 2026. https://www.ledgerinsights.com/euroclear-to-adopt-socgen-usdcv-stablecoin-for-commercial-paper-settlement/.

## One-line summary

Euroclear will use Societe Generale-FORGE's MiCA-compliant USDCV stablecoin as a secondary settlement instrument for dollar-denominated commercial paper under Project Pythagore.

## Key claims

- Euroclear has partnered with SG-FORGE to use USDCV for settling dollar-denominated commercial paper transactions under Project Pythagore, which targets tokenised negotiable EU commercial paper.
- USDCV is MiCA-compliant and regulated by the ACPR, the Banque de France's prudential supervisor, positioning SG-FORGE as a French electronic money token issuer under central bank oversight.
- Primary settlement for euro-denominated transactions uses the ECB's Project Pontes (launching September 2026) and the Banque de France's DL3S wholesale CBDC solution.
- USDCV serves as the secondary settlement mechanism for non-euro transactions, operating alongside wholesale CBDC infrastructure.
- The arrangement integrates a regulated e-money token with a central securities depository's settlement function, a novel combination for European capital markets.

## Excerpts

## Significance

- Demonstrates the first integration of a MiCA-regulated e-money token into a CSD settlement workflow, providing live evidence of MiCA's practical operation in European capital markets.
- The conjunction of USDCV (private stablecoin), Project Pontes (ECB DLT bridge), and DL3S (wholesale CBDC) illustrates the layered settlement architecture emerging in the EU.
- Relevant to the singleness-of-money doctrine: Euroclear's acceptance of a regulated stablecoin alongside wholesale CBDC tests the interoperability between private and public digital money.
- Provides a concrete EU counterpart to US GENIUS Act reserve fund developments, useful for jurisdiction-comparative analysis.
