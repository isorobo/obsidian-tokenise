# SEC Plans to Scrap Two NMS Rules That Hinder Tokenized Stock Trading via DeFi AMMs

## Citation

Ledger Insights. "SEC Plans to Scrap Two NMS Rules That Hinder Tokenized Stock Trading via DeFi AMMs". Ledger Insights, 2026-06-12. https://www.ledgerinsights.com/sec-plans-to-scrap-two-nms-rules-that-hinder-tokenized-stock-trading-via-defi-amms/.

## One-line summary

The SEC proposed rescinding Regulation NMS Rules 611 and 610(e), removing the order-protection barrier that prevents tokenised stock trading via decentralised automated market makers.

## Key claims

- The SEC proposed rescinding Rule 611 (the trade-through rule) and Rule 610(e) of Regulation NMS in a proposal dated 11 June 2026.
- Rule 611 currently prevents exchanges from executing orders at a price inferior to the best available quote on any other protected exchange.
- The proposal opens a 60-day comment period.
- SEC Chair Paul Atkins co-authored a 2005 dissent against these same rules.
- Market fragmentation has worsened: 17 national exchanges now operate versus four before NMS implementation, with no single exchange holding more than 20% market share.
- Off-exchange volume exceeded 50% in early 2025, leading the SEC to argue Rule 611 now hurts the institutional orders it was designed to protect.

## Excerpts

> "lowered the barrier to entry" by requiring market-wide connections whenever new exchanges posted protected quotes, contributing to excessive exchange proliferation and liquidity dispersion
> ~ SEC argument, as reported in article

## Significance

- Rescission of Rule 611 is a prerequisite for tokenised equities to trade on DeFi automated market makers within a compliant US framework.
- The proposal confirms that legacy market-structure rules, not only new crypto legislation, are the operative constraint on tokenised-equity market design in the US.
- The link between Rule 611 rescission and DeFi AMM trading establishes a new intersection between securities law and decentralised finance doctrine.
- Directly relevant to the vault's market-structure and securities-law corpus, alongside the tokenised-equity instrument class.
