# Why Aren't Tokenized Deposits Moving Faster?

## Citation

Ledger Insights. "Why Aren't Tokenized Deposits Moving Faster?". Ledger Insights, 16 July 2026. https://www.ledgerinsights.com/why-arent-tokenized-deposits-moving-faster/.

## One-line summary

UK and EU bank-led tokenised deposit initiatives are progressing but remain slowed by regulatory fragmentation, particularly the lack of a harmonised EU classification for tokenised deposits under MiCA.

## Key claims

- The UK's Great British Tokenised Deposits initiative, with seven participating banks, has completed its orchestration layer and plans live transactions through the fourth quarter of 2026.
- The EU's Commercial Bank Money Token project, with four participating banks, conducted its first multibank transactions in May 2026.
- Only Germany's BaFin has formally confirmed that tokenised deposits qualify as deposits under the EU's Markets in Crypto-Assets framework, leaving the rest of the EU without harmonised classification.
- UK Finance and DZ Bank representatives identify integrating tokenised money into existing bank infrastructure, not blockchain technology itself, as the harder engineering problem.
- Banks report resource scarcity from running tokenised deposits alongside multiple concurrent digital money initiatives, slowing prioritisation.

## Excerpts

> "Each firm has a different perspective... simultaneously developing their expertise in what is still quite a novel area"
> ~ Rhiannon Butterfield, UK Finance

> "The harder part is integrating tokenised money into existing bank infrastructure"
> ~ Liv Tschee-Wegert, DZ Bank

## Significance

- Documents regulatory fragmentation as the binding constraint on EU tokenised deposit adoption, with BaFin's MiCA classification standing alone against an otherwise unharmonised bloc.
- Provides bank-practitioner evidence that legacy system integration, not distributed ledger technology, is the primary adoption bottleneck for tokenised deposits.
- Cross-references two live 2026 bank consortium pilots, GBTD and CBMT, giving the wiki a direct UK-EU comparison point for tokenised deposit governance models.
- Relevant alongside the Swift shared-ledger pilot as evidence that tokenised deposit infrastructure is maturing unevenly across jurisdictions in 2026.
