# Dollarisation and monetary control: what lessons for the rise of stablecoins?

## Citation

Hofmann, Boris, Aaron Mehrotra, and Jan Paulick. "Dollarisation and monetary control: what lessons for the rise of stablecoins?". BIS Working Papers, No 1370, 21 July 2026. https://www.bis.org/publ/work1370.htm.

## One-line summary

This BIS working paper compares dollar-pegged stablecoin inflows against historical foreign-currency deposit dollarisation across more than 130 economies, and finds stablecoins share the same macro-financial drivers but evade capital controls more readily.

## Key claims

- Stablecoins create a new channel for households and firms in emerging market and developing economies to access US dollar liquidity, paralleling the historical role of foreign currency deposits.
- Historical deposit dollarisation and recent stablecoin inflows share similar macro-financial drivers, including the strength of exchange rate pass-through and sovereign or banking crises.
- Both deposit dollarisation and stablecoin dollarisation display significant persistence, and dollarisation is hard to reverse once established.
- Stablecoin flows largely escape both broad and targeted capital flow restrictions, unlike deposit dollarisation, because stablecoins circulate partly outside the regulatory perimeter.
- Moderate deposit dollarisation has historically correlated with somewhat higher inflation risk, though the paper finds little evidence of material impact on monetary policy transmission.
- The authors draw on foreign currency deposit data and dollar-pegged stablecoin inflow data spanning more than 130 economies to build the comparison.

## Significance

- Extends the wiki's stablecoin corpus beyond transaction-level and reserve-asset analysis into monetary sovereignty, connecting stablecoins directly to the dollarisation literature.
- Supplies empirical evidence, drawn from 130+ economies, that stablecoins bypass capital account controls more effectively than conventional foreign-currency deposits.
- Names the regulatory-perimeter gap as the mechanism behind stablecoin capital-control evasion, load-bearing for the wiki's cross-border-payments and monetary-policy domain nodes.
- Complements BIS WP 1340 on stablecoin-FX spillovers by shifting focus from short-run market dynamics to the structural, persistent nature of dollarisation once established.
