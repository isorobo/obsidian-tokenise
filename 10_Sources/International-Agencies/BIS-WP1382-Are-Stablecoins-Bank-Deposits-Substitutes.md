# Are stablecoins and bank deposits substitutes?

## Citation

Rashad Ahmed and Iñaki Aldasoro. "Are stablecoins and bank deposits substitutes?". BIS Working Papers No 1382, 13 October 2026. https://www.bis.org/publications/working-paper-1382-are-stablecoins-and-bank-deposits-substitutes.

## One-line summary

Using weekly US data, the paper finds that higher bank deposit rates predict slower stablecoin growth, so stablecoins and deposits behave as partial substitutes.

## Key claims

- The authors estimate how stablecoin market capitalisation responds to changes in bank deposit rates, using local projections on weekly US data from January 2019 to June 2025.
- Deposit rate increases predict slower stablecoin growth.
- To address endogeneity, the authors exploit a nonlinearity: banks reprice deposits more aggressively once the federal funds rate exceeds about 3%.
- OLS estimates imply a reduction of roughly 3 percentage points per basis point of deposit repricing, against average ten-week stablecoin growth of 15%. IV estimates are about three times larger and pass a placebo test using non-stablecoin cryptocurrencies.
- The effect is stronger for USDC than for USDT, and variation across deposit tiers gives further evidence of substitution.
- The paper concerns the allocation of funds between assets. It does not show that stablecoin growth reduces aggregate deposits or lending.
- If stablecoins grow, they could affect bank funding and monetary policy transmission.

## Significance

- Supplies causal-style evidence on the deposit-competition channel between reserve-backed stablecoins and bank deposits.
- Complements the BIS stablecoin working papers already indexed on macro effects and safe asset prices.
- Bears on the tokenised-deposit versus stablecoin debate, since deposit pricing shapes stablecoin demand.
- The data are US only, but the BIS issues the paper as an international standard-setter contribution, hence the INTL code.
