# Competing rails for crossborder payments: banks, fintechs, and stablecoins

## Citation

Wenxin Du, Catherine Huang and David Scharfstein. "Competing rails for crossborder payments: banks, fintechs, and stablecoins". BIS Working Papers No 1385, 19 October 2026. https://www.bis.org/publications/working-paper-1385-competing-rails-crossborder-payments-banks-fintechs-and-stablecoins.

## One-line summary

The paper builds harmonised all-in cost measures for cross-border payments and finds that fintech money transfer operators have cut costs most, while stablecoins are not a cure-all.

## Key claims

- The paper compares three payment rails: traditional banks (correspondent banking and SWIFT), fintech money transfer operators (MTOs), and stablecoin-based transfers.
- It builds harmonised all-in cost measures across corridors and payment sizes.
- In the authors' sample, bank FX markups ran from about 2% to 4.7%, before explicit wire fees; fintech costs are considerably lower and fall as a share of the amount sent as transfer size rises.
- Stablecoin transfers still depend on intermediaries: fiat-to-stablecoin conversion relies on crypto exchanges whose trading, conversion and withdrawal fees can raise total costs.
- Stablecoins can offer better FX pricing on some developed-to-emerging-market corridors, but the paper attributes this to capital-control distortions rather than the technology.
- Competitiveness of stablecoin rails depends on liquidity, fiat access, regulatory compliance and trust in intermediaries, not only on blockchain technology.

## Significance

- It gives an empirical cost benchmark for the claim that stablecoins improve cross-border payments, a central argument in the vault's stablecoin material.
- It complements the BIS stablecoin papers on FX spillovers and bank deposit substitution by focusing on payment-rail economics.
- The capital-control finding links stablecoin demand to regulatory arbitrage, which matters for jurisdictional comparisons.
