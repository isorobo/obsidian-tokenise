# The US dollar shortage in global banking and the international policy response

## Citation

Patrick McGuire and Goetz von Peter. "The US dollar shortage in global banking and the international policy response". BIS Working Papers, No 291, 14 October 2009. https://www.bis.org/publ/work291.htm.

## One-line summary

Reconstructing banks' worldwide consolidated balance sheets from BIS statistics, the paper shows that the post-2000 growth in European and Japanese banks' US dollar assets created structural dollar funding needs, which explains the dollar shortage of the global financial crisis and the central bank swap lines that answered it.

## Key claims

- The international provision of US dollars through central bank swap lines stands out among the policy responses to the global financial crisis.
- The authors use the BIS international banking statistics to reconstruct the worldwide consolidated balance sheets of the major national banking systems.
- The reconstruction shows how banks' international asset positions are funded across currencies and counterparties, and how their operations are structured across offices in different countries.
- A country's "national balance sheet", a residency-based measure, can be a misleading guide to where the vulnerabilities of its national banking system (or residents) lie.
- Consolidated balance sheets show that the growth since 2000 in European and Japanese banks' US dollar assets produced structural US dollar funding requirements.
- Those requirements set the stage for the dollar shortage once interbank and swap markets became impaired.

## Significance

- Background source for a concept note on the Eurodollar market and offshore dollar funding: it documents the structural, non-US-resident demand for dollar funding that sits behind the offshore dollar system.
- Predates the tokenisation theme and was added by hand as a backfill item. It fits the vault's monetary-policy and market-structure domains through its treatment of central bank swap lines and cross-border bank funding.
- Provides the pre-stablecoin baseline for later BIS work on dollar dominance and dollarisation, since stablecoins extend dollar access outside the banking system's balance sheets.
- Its consolidated-versus-residency distinction is a useful measurement caution for any analysis of where dollar liabilities and vulnerabilities sit.
