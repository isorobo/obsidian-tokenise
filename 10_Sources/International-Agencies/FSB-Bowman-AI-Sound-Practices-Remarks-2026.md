# Opening Remarks on Sound Practices for Artificial Intelligence

## Citation

Bowman, Michelle W. "Opening Remarks on Sound Practices for Artificial Intelligence". Financial Stability Board, 7 July 2026. https://www.fsb.org/2026/07/opening-remarks-on-sound-practices-for-artificial-intelligence/.

## One-line summary

Michelle Bowman, chair of the FSB Standing Committee on Supervisory and Regulatory Cooperation, opens a virtual outreach event on the FSB's AI consultation report by stressing materiality and proportionality as the organising principles for supervisory response to AI adoption.

## Key claims

- Bowman chairs the FSB workstream that produced the June 2026 consultation report on sound practices for AI adoption.
- Hern Shin Ho of the Monetary Authority of Singapore led the drafting workstream within a compressed timeframe.
- The Federal Reserve has tracked bank AI usage for nearly a decade and reports a marked recent increase across banks of all sizes.
- Managing AI risk starts with understanding the specific use case and its materiality to an institution's operations or legal obligations.
- Supervisory intensity should scale with materiality; lower-risk AI uses warrant a lighter supervisory touch.
- The sound practices must remain proportionate, since controls fit for large institutions using complex AI differ from those fit for smaller institutions.
- Consultation feedback will inform a final report due later in 2026 as a US G20 deliverable.

## Significance

- Bowman's remarks give the US supervisory gloss on the FSB's AI consultation report, showing how the Federal Reserve reads materiality and proportionality into the 12 sound practices.
- The materiality test is a candidate filter for which agentic-AI trading and settlement deployments attract heightened supervisory scrutiny under the eventual FSB standard.
- The remarks confirm the final AI report is a formal US G20 presidency deliverable, fixing the political timeline for the wiki's agentic-AI regulatory tracker.
- The proportionality framing bears on how the FSB standard will apply differently to large custodians and asset managers running AI-driven RWA platforms versus smaller platforms.
