# Global Stablecoin Arrangements: Cross-border vulnerabilities and financial stability monitoring

## Citation

Financial Stability Board. "Global Stablecoin Arrangements: Cross-border vulnerabilities and financial stability monitoring". FSB Reports to the G20, 12 October 2026. https://www.fsb.org/2026/10/global-stablecoin-arrangements-cross-border-vulnerabilities-and-financial-stability-monitoring/.

## One-line summary

The FSB sets out why global stablecoin arrangements raise cross-border vulnerabilities that no single jurisdiction can assess alone, and reviews the metrics and data sources available for monitoring them.

## Key claims

- The FSB published the note on 12 October 2026 as part of its work for the G20, alongside a Chair press release on FSB work on AI and stablecoins.
- The note summarises FSB monitoring work on global stablecoin arrangements: how they are structured, how vulnerabilities could spread, and which metrics could support financial stability monitoring.
- It reviews the data currently available on stablecoins, including the benefits and limitations of each source.
- Global stablecoins operate across multiple jurisdictions and sectors, and could become systemically important in one or more of them.
- Operating a global stablecoin can involve several types of entities, which may fall under different national authorities.
- The PDF has three sections: Introduction, cross-border vulnerabilities related to global stablecoin arrangements, and stablecoin data sources, availability and challenges.
- The PDF links back to the FSB 2023 high-level recommendations for global stablecoin arrangements and the October 2024 G20 status report on crypto-asset policy implementation. Only the web page overview was readable; the PDF body text was not extractable.

## Significance

- It is the first FSB stablecoin-specific deliverable since the October 2025 thematic review of the crypto framework, and it shifts the focus to monitoring and data gaps.
- The cross-border framing supports arguments that stablecoin oversight needs international coordination, which bears on the monetary-hierarchy and singleness-of-money themes in the vault.
- The data-source section points to gaps that the vault's later work on stablecoin reserves and market data should track.
