# Cross-Border Payments: Towards the Next Chapter

## Citation

Moloney, Martin. "Cross-Border Payments: Towards the Next Chapter". Financial Stability Board / OMFIF, 8 July 2026. https://www.fsb.org/2026/07/cross-border-payments-towards-the-next-chapter/.

## One-line summary

FSB Deputy Secretary General Martin Moloney assesses stablecoins against the FSB's cross-border payments programme, finding their current volume marginal and their likely future role as hybrid components rather than a standalone rail.

## Key claims

- Stablecoin volumes remain a very small share of total cross-border flows, estimated at less than 0.2% of total cross-border payments in 2025.
- Stablecoins' most immediate value may lie in hybrid models, integrated with bank money and interoperable foreign exchange and settlement rails.
- The cross-border payments market is dominated by participants seeking second- or third-mover advantage rather than first-mover technology adoption.
- ISO 20022 messaging standard implementation covers 77% of fast payment systems and 53% of real-time gross settlement systems, but consistent usage lags formal adoption.
- Implementation of common standards is necessary but not sufficient; the value of ISO 20022 depends on consistent usage across participants.
- The FSB faces a strategic choice between continued friction-removal, corridor-by-corridor operational planning, or deferring to private-sector technology innovators.
- Cross-border payments remain marginal to the core business model of most providers, which weakens the commercial incentive to invest in faster rails.

## Significance

- This is the FSB's most recent quantified assessment of stablecoin usage in cross-border payments, giving the wiki a current benchmark figure (under 0.2% of flows).
- The "hybrid model" framing positions stablecoins as a settlement-layer complement rather than a replacement for correspondent banking, directly relevant to the blockchain-settlement sub-topic.
- The remarks extend Moloney's April 2025 IIF speech, showing the FSB's continuing scepticism toward stablecoins as a standalone cross-border solution.
- The ISO 20022 adoption figures give a concrete infrastructure baseline against which tokenised settlement claims can be tested.
