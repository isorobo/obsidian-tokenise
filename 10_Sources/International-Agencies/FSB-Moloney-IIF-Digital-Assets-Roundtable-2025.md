# Remarks by Martin Moloney at the IIF Digital Assets Roundtable

## Citation

Moloney, Martin. "Remarks by Martin Moloney at the IIF Digital Assets Roundtable". Financial Stability Board / International Institute of Finance, 25 April 2025. https://www.fsb.org/2025/04/remarks-by-martin-moloney-at-the-iif-digital-assets-roundtable/.

## One-line summary

FSB Deputy Secretary General Martin Moloney frames tokenisation as a coordination problem rather than a financial stability threat, while characterising stablecoins as posing systemic danger requiring coordinated international regulation.

## Key claims

- Tokenisation is primarily a coordination problem within the financial sector, not a current financial stability concern.
- Profitable revenue models for early adopters in tokenised markets are absent, which is the binding constraint on adoption.
- Regulation should not be the binding constraint on tokenisation if proper data generation and legal frameworks are in place.
- Stablecoins are "incredibly dangerous" and require robust legal protections, stabilisation mechanisms, and prudential safeguards.
- The FSB has never proposed prohibiting stablecoins but insists on strong international regulatory coordination.
- Cross-border cooperation is essential yet remains the hardest problem, given crypto's global footprint.

## Significance

- Moloney's remarks distinguish FSB thinking on tokenisation from its thinking on stablecoins: different risk profiles, different regulatory urgency.
- The "coordination problem" framing is load-bearing for the vault's agentic-AI-trading and blockchain-settlement analysis, which turns on collective action barriers.
- The statement that regulation is not the binding constraint is an authoritative counter-signal to industry claims that regulation blocks adoption.
- The speech situates the October 2025 peer review in the FSB's rolling policy dialogue, establishing the sequence of FSB positions.
