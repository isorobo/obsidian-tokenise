# Sound Practices for Responsible Adoption of Artificial Intelligence (AI): Consultation Report

## Citation

Financial Stability Board. "Sound Practices for Responsible Adoption of Artificial Intelligence (AI): Consultation Report". Financial Stability Board, 10 June 2026. https://www.fsb.org/2026/06/sound-practices-for-responsible-adoption-of-artificial-intelligence-ai-consultation-report/.

## One-line summary

The FSB proposes 12 sound practices for responsible AI adoption across financial institutions, covering organisation-wide governance and the full AI lifecycle, with the aim of reducing systemic financial stability risks from unchecked AI use.

## Key claims

- Financial institutions are deploying AI to transform operations and services, but rapid adoption may amplify or introduce risks that require active management.
- The FSB proposes 12 sound practices organised into three clusters: organisation-wide AI governance, risk management across AI development stages, and cyber and third-party risk oversight.
- The practices explicitly address emerging and complex AI forms, including generative AI and agentic AI.
- Responsible AI adoption requires guardrails throughout the AI lifecycle, from development through deployment and ongoing monitoring.
- The practices are not intended to establish an international standard; they offer a menu that institutions can adapt to their context.
- The report draws on prior FSB AI assessments from 2017 and 2024, positioning this as the next step in the FSB's AI monitoring programme.
- A final report incorporating consultation responses is scheduled for October 2026.

## Significance

- The FSB's explicit treatment of agentic AI as a distinct risk category marks the first FSB-level governance framework targeted at autonomous AI agents operating within financial institutions.
- The report directly connects AI governance to financial stability, establishing the FSB's supervisory interest in AI-driven trading, AI-assisted settlement, and related automated processes.
- The October 2026 final report will carry weight as FSB soft law; this consultation is the authoritative reference for understanding the likely final standard.
