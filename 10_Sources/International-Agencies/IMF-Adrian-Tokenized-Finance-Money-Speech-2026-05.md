# Tokenized Finance and Money

## Citation

Adrian, Tobias. "Tokenized Finance and Money." Remarks at the Third Conference on Stablecoins and Tokenization, Federal Reserve Banks of Boston and New York, 2026-05-11. https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/05/11/sp051126-tokenized-finance-and-money.

## One-line summary

In this keynote speech, IMF Financial Counsellor Tobias Adrian presents a five-pillar policy roadmap for tokenised finance, arguing that safe settlement assets, consistent cross-activity regulation, and override mechanisms for systemically important smart contracts are preconditions for financial stability.

## Key claims

- Tokenisation eliminates the temporal buffers (end-of-day settlement, batch processing) that have historically given regulators time to intervene, making continuous atomic settlement a financial stability risk as well as an efficiency gain.
- Stablecoins are the system's most vulnerable link: functional in calm conditions but susceptible to self-reinforcing runs, with monthly transaction volume reaching $1.8 trillion by early 2026.
- Three broad regulatory frameworks are now taking shape: the EU MiCA Regulation (transitional provisions through July 2026), the UK draft stablecoin regime, and the US GENIUS Act in its rulemaking phase.
- Adrian's five-pillar policy roadmap calls for (1) anchoring settlement in safe assets such as wCBDC, (2) consistent regulation across similar activities regardless of form, (3) adapting central bank liquidity tools to automated environments, (4) mandatory audit and override mechanisms for systemically important smart contracts, and (5) international coordination on cross-border tokenised flows.
- Legal mandates for financial stability must prevail over automated execution: no smart contract should be beyond the reach of regulatory override in a systemic crisis.
- The speech addresses four core questions: what tokenisation implies for the financial sector, what settles tokenised transactions, what macro-financial implications stablecoins carry for both issuing and recipient jurisdictions, and the current state of the regulatory landscape.
