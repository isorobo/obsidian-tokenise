# Understanding Stablecoins

## Citation

Adrian, Tobias, Parma Bains, Marianne Bechara, Eugenio Cerutti, Stephanie Forte, Federico Grinberg, Alessandro Gullo, Martina Hengge, Agnija Jekabsone, Kathleen Kao, Tommaso Mancini Griffoli, Soledad Martinez Peria, Marcello Miccoli, Marco Reuter, and Nobuyasu Sugimoto. "Understanding Stablecoins." IMF Departmental Paper No. 25/09, International Monetary Fund, Monetary and Capital Markets Department, December 2025. https://www.imf.org/en/publications/departmental-papers/issues/2025/12/02/understanding-stablecoins-570602.

## One-line summary

The paper provides the IMF's comprehensive reference account of fiat-backed stablecoins, covering their characteristics, market growth, potential benefits, macrofinancial risks, and the regulatory frameworks that jurisdictions have enacted or proposed.

## Key claims

- Stablecoins are issued on distributed ledgers and aim to maintain a fixed parity against a reference currency; most are fiat-backed, denominated in United States dollars, and backed 1:1 with short-term liquid financial assets.
- Stablecoin issuance doubled in the two years to 2025, driven primarily by their use as a bridge between volatile unbacked crypto assets and fiat currencies, with expanding use in cross-border payments.
- Unlike tokenised central bank money or tokenised deposits, stablecoins offer more limited redemption rights and could pose macrofinancial stability risks through runs, reserve-asset impairment, bank disintermediation, and currency substitution in emerging market economies.
- Stablecoins differ from tokenised money market funds in that they do not pay returns; they share with unbacked crypto assets the feature of peer-to-peer transferability on public blockchains.
- The regulatory landscape is fragmented: Japan, the European Union (MiCA), the United States (GENIUS Act), and the United Kingdom each take distinct approaches to reserve requirements, redemption rights, AML/CFT obligations, and issuer licensing.
- The paper consolidates rather than extends IMF policy lines; it serves as the authoritative IMF reference document for policymakers designing or reviewing stablecoin regulatory frameworks.

## Excerpts

> "Stablecoins are part of the broader trend of asset tokenization—the representation of assets on distributed ledgers, providing a new infrastructure and process through which assets are recorded, issued, and transferred—although stablecoins differ from traditional and tokenized assets in several key ways."
> ~ Executive Summary, p. 3

> "Unlike central bank money and deposits (and their potential tokenized form), stablecoins could offer less stability if regulatory frameworks do not address the potential market and liquidity risk of their backing assets and generally provide—at the current juncture—more limited redemption rights."
> ~ Executive Summary, p. 3

> "Stablecoins could also carry significant risks—in the absence of adequate regulations and backstops—that would be more pronounced in countries with weaker macroeconomic fundamentals and institutions."
> ~ Executive Summary, p. 3

## Significance

- The first full IMF Departmental Paper devoted to stablecoins synthesises all prior IMF stablecoin analysis into a single reference document, making it the baseline for any comparative regulatory study.
- The comparative regulatory table (Table 2) covering Japan, EU, US, and UK is the most comprehensive cross-jurisdictional stablecoin regulatory comparison published by any international body in 2025.
- The paper's risk taxonomy (runs, reserve-asset impairment, bank disintermediation, currency substitution, capital-flow reshaping) directly maps onto the macroprudential doctrine thread in this vault.
- Its position as an IMF Departmental Paper means it carries institutional weight comparable to Article IV consultation analysis, making it load-bearing for any policy advocacy or regulatory reform argument.
