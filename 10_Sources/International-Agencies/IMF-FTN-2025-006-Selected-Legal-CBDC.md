# Selected Legal Considerations for Central Bank Digital Currencies

## Citation

Bechara, Marianne, Adrian Dumitrescu-Pasecinic, and Tomoyuki Kubota. "Selected Legal Considerations for Central Bank Digital Currencies." IMF FinTech Notes, NOTE/2025/006, November 2025. https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/ftn063/2025/english/ftnea2025006.pdf.

## One-line summary

The note maps the public-law foundations that a retail CBDC requires, covering the central bank's mandate to issue currency, operate a payment platform, regulate intermediaries, and manage legal relationships with users and service providers.

## Key claims

- Retail CBDC must constitute legal tender under domestic monetary law; central banks require an explicit statutory basis to issue it, operate a two-tier distribution platform, and regulate private intermediaries.
- The legal relationship between the central bank and CBDC users depends on the design model: an "asset link" model gives users a direct legal claim on the central bank, whereas a "technological link" model does not, with significantly different insolvency implications.
- Central banks that deploy CBDC payment platforms face a dual role as operator and regulator, creating potential conflicts of interest that legal frameworks must address.
- Mandatory participation requirements for private intermediaries and compulsory wallet portability raise competition-law and administrative-law questions distinct from existing payment-service licensing regimes.
- A two-tier distribution model requires clear legal rules governing the intermediary's obligations to CBDC users, including safekeeping, settlement finality, and AML/CFT compliance.
- No single legal template suits all jurisdictions; the appropriate framework depends on the chosen CBDC design features and the existing domestic legal tradition.

## Significance

- Provides the clearest IMF framework to date on the public-law preconditions for retail CBDC, making it the reference note for any jurisdiction assessing its central bank legislation.
- The two-tier intermediary analysis directly informs how tokenised-deposit and e-money-token regulation interacts with CBDC infrastructure.
- The dual operator-regulator conflict analysis is relevant to any jurisdiction considering central bank-run DLT infrastructure for tokenised asset settlement.
- Complements the private-law sister note (2025/003) to form a complete IMF legal framework for CBDC design.
