# Central Bank Exploration of Tokenized Reserves

## Citation

Kunaratskul, Tansaya, Ashley Lannquist, André Reslow, and Nicolas Xuan-Yi Zhang. "Central Bank Exploration of Tokenized Reserves." IMF FinTech Notes, NOTE/2025/011, November 2025. https://www.imf.org/en/publications/fintech-notes/issues/2025/11/18/central-bank-exploration-of-tokenized-reserves-571876.

## One-line summary

The note defines tokenised reserves as DLT-settled wholesale central bank money, surveys four operating models and four strategic approaches, and compares tokenised reserves against alternative settlement solutions for tokenised asset markets.

## Key claims

- "Tokenised reserves" are preferred over "wholesale CBDC" because the term emphasises that only the technology changes; the underlying instrument remains a direct central bank liability accessible only to licensed financial institutions.
- Central banks explore tokenised reserves to preserve the risk-free, atomic settlement role of central bank money within emerging tokenised asset ecosystems.
- Four operating models exist for a single ledger: integration, distribution, separation, and hybrid; the choice determines the central bank's degree of control over issuance, access, data, and system continuity.
- Tokenisation does not fundamentally alter a central bank's ability to implement monetary policy, but programmable liquidity management may require updates to governance, legal frameworks, and risk management.
- Alternative solutions - RTGS links, omnibus accounts, and privately issued tokenised money - each offer different trade-offs in risk, cost, programmability, and alignment with policy objectives; no single solution dominates.
- Central banks may adopt four strategic postures: inaction, wait-and-see, enabling private-sector innovation, or acting as a public-sector catalyst; the appropriate choice depends on market readiness and institutional capacity.

## Excerpts

> "Central banks explore tokenized reserves as a way to preserve the safety, liquidity, and policy role of central bank money within tokenized ecosystems by enabling risk-free settlement and support for more efficient, automated, and resilient wholesale payment systems."
> ~ §I Introduction, p. 4

> "The term 'tokenized reserves' is preferred because it emphasizes that the only change lies in the technology settling reserves as they already exist in digital form."
> ~ §I Introduction, p. 4

> "Although tokenization may not fundamentally affect central banks' ability to implement monetary policy, it could introduce enhancements such as programmable or automated liquidity management, which may require updates to governance, legal frameworks, and risk management."
> ~ §I Introduction, p. 4

> "Policymakers should also recognize that DLT, including issuance of reserves, is not a solution for structural issues but a complement to strong institutions, sound policies, and robust legal frameworks."
> ~ §VII Conclusion, p. 32

## Significance

- Sets the authoritative IMF framework for evaluating wholesale tokenised reserves, directly applicable to the wiki's blockchain-settlement and CBDC threads.
- The four-strategic-posture model gives central banks a decision tree for DLT adoption, making the note a primary reference for jurisdictions considering Project Ensemble (HKMA), Project Guardian (MAS), and Project Agora (BIS) equivalents.
- The alternative-solutions comparison table is the most systematic cross-instrument analysis of settlement infrastructure for tokenised asset markets published by any international body in 2025.
- The note was prepared with financial support from the Government of Japan, reflecting G7-level interest in the standardisation agenda.
