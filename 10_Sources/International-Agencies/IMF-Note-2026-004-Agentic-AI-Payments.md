# How Agentic AI Will Reshape Payments

## Citation

Davidovic, Sonja, and Hervé Tourpe. "How Agentic AI Will Reshape Payments." IMF Notes 2026/004, International Monetary Fund, Washington, DC, 2026-04-24. https://www.imf.org/en/publications/imf-notes/issues/2026/04/22/how-agentic-ai-will-reshape-payments-575560.

## One-line summary

The IMF frames agentic AI in payments through a three-layer model separating intent orchestration, authorisation control, and settlement execution, arguing that probabilistic AI decision-making must remain structurally isolated from deterministic payment rails.

## Key claims

- Agentic AI systems can interpret objectives, plan multi-step actions, and initiate payments with limited human intervention, shifting transaction initiation from explicit human instruction to agent-mediated decision-making.
- A central architectural tension exists between probabilistic AI reasoning and the deterministic requirements of payment infrastructures such as RTGS systems, card networks, and distributed ledgers.
- The three-layer model proposed separates (1) intent formation and orchestration, (2) authorisation and control, and (3) settlement; AI should operate only in layers one and two, with layer three remaining deterministic.
- DLT-based payment environments (stablecoins, wCBDC) carry a heightened risk of legal finality gaps when agentic systems execute on-chain transactions without statutory settlement recognition.
- Key regulatory gaps include the absence of know-your-agent frameworks, authentication standards for AI-initiated transactions, and cross-border supervisory coordination covering agent-mediated payment flows.
- Industry experimentation is already underway: OpenAI's Agentic Commerce Protocol, Amazon's "Buy for Me," and Google's Universal Commerce Protocol each delegate payment initiation to AI agents.

## Excerpts

> "Agentic AI systems can interpret objectives, plan multistep actions, and interact with digital services with limited human intervention. In payments, this shift could move transaction initiation from explicitly human instructions toward agent-mediated decision making."
> ~ Executive Summary, p. 4

> "The central issue is not whether AI should be used in payments—as AI has already been used for more than four decades—but how to keep uncertain, probabilistic decision making separate from automatic payment execution."
> ~ p. 8

> "In DLT environments used by stablecoins or central bank digital currencies, ERC 4337, launched in 2023, provides smart contract wallet execution for authorized transactions."
> ~ p. 12

> "Given the non-deterministic nature of generative models, agentic AI systems should not directly execute irreversible transactions without structural safeguards. The architectural separation of decision-making and execution layers can act as an important mitigating factor."
> ~ p. 19

## Significance

- Provides the first IMF-level policy framework for agentic AI in payments, directly relevant to the wiki's agentic-AI-trading domain and DLT settlement analysis.
- The three-layer architecture maps onto tokenised and CBDC settlement environments, making it a foundational reference for the wiki's blockchain-settlement and stablecoin nodes.
- Identifies the legal finality gap in DLT-based agentic execution as an unresolved risk requiring cross-border regulatory coordination.
- Situates industry deployments (OpenAI, Amazon, Google) within a supervisory risk taxonomy, providing a primary IMF anchor for monitoring agentic commerce developments.
