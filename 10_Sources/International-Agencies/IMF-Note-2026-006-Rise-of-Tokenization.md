# The Rise of Tokenization: Deciphering New Trends in Payments and Asset Tokenization

## Citation

Adrian, Tobias, Yaiza Cabedo, and Tommaso Mancini-Griffoli. "The Rise of Tokenization: Deciphering New Trends in Payments and Asset Tokenization." IMF Note 2026/006, International Monetary Fund, Washington, DC, 2026-07-01. https://www.imf.org/en/publications/imf-notes/issues/2026/07/01/the-rise-of-tokenization-deciphering-new-trends-in-payments-and-asset-tokenization-577138.

## One-line summary

This IMF Note applies a three-layer infrastructure, asset, and services framework to catalogue 2026 tokenisation trends, arguing that stablecoins fail the traditional test of money while banks and native crypto firms converge on hybrid permissioned and permissionless ledger architectures.

## Key claims

- Tokenisation is gaining momentum in financial markets and carries significant implications for market structure, risk management, and financial stability.
- A three-layer framework, infrastructure, asset, and services, structures the analysis and applies equally to traditional and tokenised financial architectures.
- Banks and native crypto firms are converging on hybrid governance: JPMorgan Coin now runs on Coinbase's public chain Base, UBS has joined Stripe's Tempo testnet, and Societe Generale's CoinVertible stablecoin issues across four public chains.
- Swift is building an open-source, EVM-compatible ledger on which member banks retain full authority over keys and settlement, while JPMorgan Chase, Bank of America, Citigroup, and Wells Fargo will clear tokenised deposits through a new Clearing House network from early 2027.
- Three interoperability architectures exist: single ledger, compatible ledger, and common ledger models, each carrying distinct trade-offs between functionality, governance, and concentration risk.
- Stablecoins do not meet the traditional criteria of money because end users can transfer them peer to peer without a direct contractual relationship to the issuer, unlike bank deposits.
- Loss-absorption options for stablecoin issuers include prefunded default funds, mutualised risk-management arrangements akin to central counterparty clearing, and calibrated access to central bank liquidity.

## Excerpts

## Significance

- Extends NOTE/2026/001's structural-shift thesis with a granular single, compatible, and common ledger taxonomy, giving the wiki its clearest primary-source framework for interoperability architecture.
- Sharpens the singleness-of-money doctrine node by setting out precisely why peer-to-peer transferability places stablecoins closer to tokenised securities than to bank deposits.
- Documents dated 2026 institutional migrations, JPMorgan Coin on Base, Societe Generale's CoinVertible, and The Clearing House's 2027 tokenised-deposit network, giving the wiki concrete evidence of bank adoption.
- Positions Swift's EVM-compatible ledger as a live case study of hybrid permissioned and permissionless governance, relevant to the wiki's market-infrastructure domain.
