# Stablecoins: Promise, Risks, and Policy Choices for Emerging Markets

## Citation

Katz, Dan. "Stablecoins: Promise, Risks, and Policy Choices for Emerging Markets." Remarks at the University of Cape Town, International Monetary Fund, 2026-08-07. https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/08/07/sp080726-stablecoins-emerging-markets-dan-katz.

## One-line summary

The IMF's First Deputy Managing Director warns that domestic stablecoin launches in emerging markets can paradoxically deepen demand for dollar-pegged tokens, and urges regulators to start collecting transaction data before frameworks are complete.

## Key claims

- Users gravitate toward dollar-pegged stablecoins for their superior liquidity, network effects, and cross-border acceptance.
- A country that launches a domestic stablecoin can inadvertently boost demand for the dollar-backed tokens it was meant to compete with.
- Deploying local-currency and dollar-backed stablecoins on the same blockchain framework lowers the practical barriers to conversion between them.
- Users can exchange between stablecoin types through decentralised exchanges, liquidity pools, or peer-to-peer swaps, bypassing capital controls.
- Emerging market central banks face capital flight risk, reduced incentive to hold depreciating local currency, and complications for monetary policy transmission.
- Regulators should bring stablecoin deposits, withdrawals, and on-chain foreign exchange routes under oversight to protect monetary sovereignty and financial stability.
- Data collection on stablecoin transactions should start immediately and should not wait for a complete regulatory framework.

## Excerpts

> "Data collection should not wait for perfect regulation."
> ~ Remarks at the University of Cape Town, 2026-08-07

## Significance

- Extends the IMF's stablecoin corpus from systemic-stability framing (WP/26/74, WP/26/144) to emerging-market monetary sovereignty, a distinct policy angle not yet covered on this channel.
- Names a concrete transmission mechanism, shared-blockchain conversion between local and dollar-pegged stablecoins, by which domestic stablecoin issuance can accelerate dollarisation rather than contain it.
- Gives the wiki a primary-source articulation of the "regulate first, perfect the framework later" data-collection posture, relevant to comparative regulatory-timing analysis across jurisdictions.
- Complements NOTE/2026/006's cross-border payments layer by focusing on the currency-substitution risk facing emerging market central banks specifically.
