# AI and the Future of Payments Policy

## Citation

Katz, Dan. "AI and the Future of Payments Policy". IMF Speeches (Sibos 2026), 28 September 2026. https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/09/28/sp092826-ai-and-the-future-of-payments-policy.

## One-line summary

The IMF First Deputy Managing Director argues that AI agents will reshape payments, that tokenised and programmable infrastructure suits them, and that regulation should be proportionate, targeted at demonstrated market failures and internationally coordinated.

## Key claims

- If AI is used as widely as proponents expect, much economic activity will run through AI agents rather than people, and the current payments system was not built for that.
- Agents can compare fees, exchange rates and execution quality continuously and route each payment to the most efficient provider, which raises competition.
- Agents can automate regulatory reporting, customer due diligence and sanctions screening and monitor positions against requirements in real time.
- The financial architecture needs to become more digital, interoperable and programmable, and tokenisation is described as promising infrastructure.
- Stablecoins need backing by safe and liquid assets to keep a stable value and work as a payment instrument.
- Cross-border payments move nearly USD 1 quadrillion a year, so small efficiency gains carry large benefits.
- New regulation should be proportionate, focused and internationally coordinated.
