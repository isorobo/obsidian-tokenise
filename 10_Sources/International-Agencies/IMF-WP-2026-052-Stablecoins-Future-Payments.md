# Stablecoins and the Future of Payments: Evidence from Financial Markets

## Citation

Copestake, Alexander, Cage Englander, Maria Soledad Martinez Peria, and Germán Villegas-Bauer. "Stablecoins and the Future of Payments: Evidence from Financial Markets." IMF Working Paper WP/26/52, International Monetary Fund, Research Department, 2026-03-20. https://www.imf.org/en/publications/wp/issues/2026/03/20/stablecoins-and-the-future-of-payments-evidence-from-financial-markets-574831.

## One-line summary

Using high-frequency stock market data around congressional votes on the GENIUS Act, this paper finds that US legislation supporting stablecoins in payments reduced the market value of incumbent payment firms by 18%, or approximately $300 billion, consistent with markets expecting stablecoins to compete directly with established payment providers.

## Key claims

- The GENIUS Act created the first US federal regulatory framework for "payment stablecoins," requiring 100% backing with liquid assets and monthly public reserve disclosures.
- Passage of the GENIUS Act reduced the market capitalisation of listed incumbent payment firms by approximately 18% or $300 billion relative to other financial firms.
- The competitive impact is largest for cross-border payment incumbents, smallest for firms protected by strong network effects, and attenuated for incumbents already offering crypto-related services.
- The GENIUS Act's effect exceeds that of other recent pro-competitive regulatory shocks to the payments sector in proportional magnitude.
- Fewer than 10% of on-chain stablecoin transactions reflect genuine user-to-user transfers; blockchain data alone cannot establish whether stablecoin flows constitute payments for goods and services.
- Financial market reactions to technology shocks have a history of predictive power, making the stock-price event study a credible forward-looking measure of stablecoin adoption expectations.

## Excerpts

> "Using high-frequency variation in stock prices, we estimate that U.S. legislation supporting the use of stablecoins in payments reduced the market value of listed incumbent payment firms by 18% or approximately $300 billion, consistent with stablecoins increasing competition in the payments sector."
> ~ Abstract, p. ii

> "The GENIUS Act defines a 'payment stablecoin' as a digital asset that is, or is designed to be, used as a means of payment or settlement; and the issuer of which is obligated to convert, redeem, or repurchase for a fixed amount of monetary value."
> ~ p. 4 fn. 5

> "Incumbent payment firms' stock returns are approximately 1% lower than those of other financial firms in the five trading hours after the vote. These differences in returns are statistically significant at the 1% level and equate to approximately $21.5 billion of lost market capitalization."
> ~ p. 4

## Significance

- Provides the first empirical, market-based evidence that financial markets treat stablecoins as a genuine competitive threat to incumbent payment infrastructure.
- The cross-border payment finding is directly load-bearing for the wiki's cross-border-payments domain: it suggests stablecoins are expected to disrupt correspondent banking and FX payment rails most severely.
- Supports the wiki's policy-analysis thread on the GENIUS Act by quantifying the market's assessment of the Act's competitive consequences.
- The methodology complements on-chain blockchain data approaches by using stock market event studies, providing an independent validation signal for the wiki's stablecoin coverage.
