# Making Stablecoins Stable

## Citation

Li, Bo, Tommaso Mancini Griffoli, Marcello Miccoli, Brandon Tan, and Longmei Zhang. "Making Stablecoins Stable." IMF Working Paper WP/26/74, International Monetary Fund, Monetary and Capital Markets Department, 2026-04-10. https://www.imf.org/en/publications/wp/issues/2026/04/10/making-stablecoins-stable-575348.

## One-line summary

Using a Diamond-Dybvig bank-run model, this IMF working paper shows that unregulated stablecoin issuers hold risky assets to maximise profit, creating run risk, and that requiring safe-asset backing paired with alternative revenue sources for issuers restores a socially optimal equilibrium.

## Key claims

- An unregulated stablecoin market produces a suboptimal private equilibrium: issuers hold risky assets to maximise profits, which raises run risk and may cause stablecoins to trade below par.
- Run risk prevents stablecoins from satisfying the "no-questions-asked" (NQA) criterion required for money, as defined by Holmstrom (2015).
- A social planner can improve outcomes by requiring full backing with safe assets (such as central bank reserves in a narrow-bank setting) while also enabling alternative revenue streams for issuers.
- Policy levers available to regulators include reserve remuneration by central banks and frameworks permitting payment data monetisation.
- The GENIUS Act (enacted July 2025) and MiCA each require safe-asset backing and regular reserve disclosure, broadly consistent with the paper's prescriptions.
- The stablecoin market grew 50% over 2025, reaching $300 billion by year-end, concentrated in US dollar-pegged instruments backed by short-term government debt.

## Significance

- Supplies the formal theoretical foundation for safe-asset backing requirements in stablecoin regulation, grounding the wiki's policy analysis with a rigorous welfare model.
- Directly informs the wiki's assessment of the GENIUS Act and MiCA reserve requirements by showing the economic rationale for 100% liquid asset backing.
- Identifies the tradeoff between stability and issuance incentive as the central design challenge for stablecoin regulation, relevant to the wiki's stablecoins and monetary-policy domains.
- Reinforces the wiki's theme of "singleness of money" by demonstrating that run risk structurally excludes unregulated stablecoins from serving as true money.
