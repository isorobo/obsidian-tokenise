# UNIDROIT Principles on Digital Assets and Private Law: Implementation Strategy Update 2025

## Citation

UNIDROIT Secretariat. "Implementation Status and Promotion Strategy for UNIDROIT Instruments: UNIDROIT Principles on Digital Assets and Private Law". UNIDROIT Governing Council, 105th Session (C.D. (105) 21), Rome, 20-23 May 2025. https://www.unidroit.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/C.D.-105-21-Implementation-Strategy-Principles-on-Digital-Assets-and-Private-Law.pdf.

## One-line summary

UNIDROIT reports that its Digital Assets and Private Law Principles are influencing legislation across 25 US jurisdictions, the DIFC, China, and Italy, and sets out its 2025 strategy to expand adoption across Asia, Africa, and Latin America.

## Key claims

- Twenty-five US jurisdictions have enacted UCC Article 12 amendments on "controllable electronic records", aligning the control standard with DAPL Principle 6.
- The Dubai International Financial Centre's Digital Assets Law 2024 adopts the DAPL approach to "control" as its foundation for proprietary rights in digital assets.
- The IMF published a March 2025 paper on the private law aspects of token-based CBDCs that expressly acknowledges UNIDROIT's work in this field.
- The HCCH's March 2025 CGAP meeting mandated a new Experts' Group on private international law issues of digital tokens, with UNIDROIT appointed as observer.
- UNCITRAL's 58th Commission session in July 2025 is expected to address secured transactions using new types of assets, creating potential overlap with the DAPL Principles that UNIDROIT must manage.
- UNIDROIT's Expert Committee identified tokenised receivables as a potential future work item under DAPL Principle 5.

## Excerpts

> "US domestic law continues to be amended to reflect the approach embodied in the DAPL Principles, via enactment of amendments to the Uniform Commercial Code (UCC) ... a new Article 12 addresses transactions involving certain types of digital assets referred to as 'controllable electronic records', with Section 12-105 defining 'control' consistently with DAPL Principle 6."
> ~ Section II, paragraph 4

> "In March 2025, the International Monetary Fund (IMF) published a paper on the Private Law Aspects of Token-Based Central Bank Digital Currencies, which provides guidance on transactions in digital assets and specifically takes account of the work performed by UNIDROIT in this area."
> ~ Section II, paragraph 5

> "The Expert Committee ... discussed: ... (iii) possible future work on linked assets in relation to DAPL Principle 5 and to tokenised receivables."
> ~ Section III, paragraph 12

> "The Secretariat will liaise with the recently established Digital Assets Legislators Global Forum, which aims to support legislative approaches to digital assets, providing a platform for legislators to connect across jurisdictions and foster dialogue."
> ~ Section II, paragraph 10

## Significance

- This document is the primary 2025 status report on the DAPL Principles' adoption: it establishes the current global legislative map for private-law treatment of digital assets.
- It confirms that "control" as the proprietary gateway for digital assets is now operative in 25 US jurisdictions, the DIFC, Italy, and Argentina - making the doctrine multi-jurisdictional.
- The reference to tokenised receivables as a future work item signals a concrete pipeline extension from current DAPL scope into structured finance and supply-chain finance.
- The document's tracking of HCCH and UNCITRAL coordination is the authoritative source on fragmentation risk in the international digital-asset private-law framework.
