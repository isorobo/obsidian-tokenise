# Memorandum on the Scope and Direction of the Tokenisation Annexe to the UNIDROIT Principles on the Legal Nature of Verified Carbon Credits

## Citation

Tosato, Andrea. "Memorandum on the Scope and Direction of the Tokenisation Annexe". UNIDROIT Working Group on the Legal Nature of Verified Carbon Credits, Eighth Session, Study LXXXVI – W.G.8 – Doc. 6, Rome, 15-17 April 2026. https://www.unidroit.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Study-LXXXVI-W.G.8-Doc.-6-Tokenisation-Annexe-Memo.pdf.

## One-line summary

This Drafting Committee memorandum frames the legal analysis for a Tokenisation Annexe to the UNIDROIT VCC Principles, identifying four distinct tokenisation scenarios and concluding that private ordering alone cannot give tokens proprietary effect over underlying carbon credits.

## Key claims

- "Tokenisation" is not a term of art with a settled legal meaning; the memo identifies four legally distinct scenarios, ranging from permissioned DLT as registry infrastructure to fully disintermediated on-chain VCC creation.
- The DAPL Principles are technology-neutral and govern the token as a digital asset, but deliberately leave the legal effect of any link between the token and the underlying VCC to "other law" — creating a gap that the Annexe is designed to address.
- Private parties cannot, by agreement alone, create a proprietary right in a VCC by issuing a token; the numerus clausus principle requires law to authorise any new category of property right enforceable against third parties.
- Scenario Three — a token embodying and transferring proprietary rights in a VCC — would require new substantive principles and a prior policy mandate from the Working Group.
- Scenario Four — VCCs created and managed on a decentralised public ledger without an identifiable registry operator — is structurally incompatible with the current Principles framework.

## Excerpts

> "The central submission of this memorandum is that 'tokenisation' is not a term of art with a settled legal meaning, and that the Annexe cannot be responsibly drafted without first identifying and differentiating among the distinct phenomena that the term may describe."
> ~ §I, para. 2

> "Private parties cannot, by agreement among themselves, create a new form of proprietary right that the law does not recognise."
> ~ §III, para. 13

> "A transferee of the token may acquire contractual rights against the person who issued it, but the transferee does not, by virtue of acquiring the token, acquire a proprietary interest in the underlying VCC that is enforceable against the world."
> ~ §III, para. 13

> "The distinction is of particular consequence in insolvency: a token holder who possesses only contractual rights against the issuer will rank as an unsecured creditor in the issuer's insolvency."
> ~ §III, para. 13

## Significance

- Provides the foundational doctrinal taxonomy for tokenised carbon credits at international law level, distinguishing contractual from proprietary token arrangements.
- Confirms that the gap between the DAPL Principles and VCC law is structural, not accidental, and must be addressed legislatively rather than by private ordering.
- Draws directly on the DAPL Principle 4 "linked assets" framework, establishing inter-instrument coherence across UNIDROIT's digital asset corpus.
- Identifies insolvency treatment as the pivotal practical test of whether a token confers true proprietary rights over an underlying VCC.
