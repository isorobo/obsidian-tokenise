# Draft UNIDROIT Principles on the Legal Nature of Verified Carbon Credits (Ninth Session Text)

## Citation

UNIDROIT Drafting Committee. "Draft UNIDROIT Principles on the Legal Nature of Verified Carbon Credits". UNIDROIT Working Group on the Legal Nature of Verified Carbon Credits, Ninth Session, Study LXXXVI – W.G.9 – Doc. 3, Rome (remote), 16 June 2026. https://www.unidroit.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Study-LXXXVI-W.G.9-Doc.-3-Draft-Principles-and-Commentary.pdf.

## One-line summary

This is the full black-letter text and commentary of the draft VCC Principles considered at the ninth Working Group session, including the property rule in Principle 3, the creation-at-registration rule in Principle 5, and a bracketed tokenisation passage pending the Annexe.

## Key claims

- Principle 3(1) states that a VCC can be the subject of proprietary rights, with "proprietary rights" defined broadly to include both proprietary interests and rights with proprietary effects.
- Principle 5(1) fixes the moment a VCC comes into existence at registration, when a unique identifier is credited to a registry account, establishing the creation-time snapshot that grounds the later revocation-only-for-fraud approach.
- Principle 3(4) preserves "other law" as the governing framework for most proprietary questions, with the Principles displacing domestic law only where they expressly say so.
- The instrument runs to 23 numbered Principles across nine sections, covering scope, applicable law, creation, transfer, retirement and cancellation, illegality, registry, custody, secured transactions, and insolvency.
- The Introduction's bracketed Tokenisation section distinguishes native on-chain VCCs from VCCs that mirror an existing registry record, and states that tokenisation does not automatically confer legal rights over the underlying VCC.
- The entire tokenisation passage in the Introduction is marked as provisional text, subject to the Working Group's separate development of a Tokenisation Annexe.

## Excerpts

> "A VCC can be the subject of proprietary rights."
> ~ Principle 3(1)

> "A VCC comes into existence when it is recorded in a VCC registry that the VCC or a block of VCCs has been credited to an account in that VCC registry."
> ~ Principle 5(1)

> "Issuing a tokenised VCC does not automatically confer legal rights to the relevant original VCC and dealing with the original VCC does not automatically feed data to the tokenised VCC."
> ~ Introduction, para. 59

> "[The above section is subject to the Working Group's consideration and development of a Tokenisation annexe]"
> ~ Introduction, editorial note following para. 59

## Significance

- Provides the operative black-letter text of the draft VCC Principles as they stood immediately before Governing Council authorisation of public consultation, the primary legal source for the instrument's substantive content.
- Fixes the creation-time snapshot rule in Principle 5 as the textual anchor for the property law architecture confirmed at this session.
- Distinguishes native on-chain VCC creation from registry-mirroring tokenisation directly in the black-letter Introduction, marking a live doctrinal fault line for tokenised carbon markets.
- Establishes the authoritative baseline text against which future Drafting Committee iterations and public consultation responses will be measured.
