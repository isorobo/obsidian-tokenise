# Draft UNIDROIT Principles on the Legal Nature of Verified Carbon Credits (For Consultation)

## Citation

UNIDROIT Drafting Committee. "Draft UNIDROIT Principles on the Legal Nature of Verified Carbon Credits". UNIDROIT Working Group on the Legal Nature of Verified Carbon Credits, Study LXXXVI, Public Consultation Text, July 2026. https://www.unidroit.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Study-LXXXVI-Draft-Principles-on-the-Legal-Nature-of-VCCs-for-Consultation.pdf.

## One-line summary

This is the full 25-Principle black-letter text and commentary of the VCC Principles as released for the July to September 2026 public consultation, superseding the ninth session draft.

## Key claims

- The consultation text expands the instrument to 25 numbered Principles across nine sections, up from the 23 Principles in the ninth session draft.
- Principle 1 confines the instrument to private law, expressly excluding licensing, intellectual property, and consumer protection matters.
- Principle 5(1) retains the registration-based creation-time rule and adds new sub-paragraphs distinguishing a registered holder acting for itself from one holding as custodian for a client.
- The Introduction's Tokenisation section (paragraphs 58 to 62) sets out two models, native on-chain VCCs and registry-mirroring tokenised VCCs, and remains bracketed as provisional pending a separate Tokenisation Annexe.
- The Tokenisation section cites Carbon Plant as an example of native token issuance and J.P. Morgan Kinexys as an example of registry-mirroring tokenisation.
- The Introduction's transition rules section flags an open drafting question on whether to retain detailed grace-period provisions for existing secured transactions.
- The instrument states that tokenisation does not automatically confer legal rights over the underlying VCC and that dealing with the underlying VCC does not automatically update the tokenised record.

## Excerpts

> "These Principles address the private law relating to verified carbon credits."
> ~ Principle 1, Scope

> "A VCC comes into existence when it is recorded in a VCC registry that the VCC or a block of VCCs has been credited to an account in that VCC registry."
> ~ Principle 5(1)

> "Tokenisation in carbon markets uses blockchain to turn traditional carbon credits into digital, tradable assets with the potential for improving transparency, liquidity, automation and accessibility. There are two broad models: (i) native tokens, being VCCs created directly onto a blockchain; and (ii) tokenised VCCs that mirror an existing registry-based VCC."
> ~ Introduction, para. 58

> "Issuing a tokenised VCC does not automatically confer legal rights to the relevant original VCC and dealing with the original VCC does not automatically feed data to the tokenised VCC."
> ~ Introduction, para. 62

> "[The above section is subject to the Working Group's consideration and development of a Tokenisation annexe]"
> ~ Introduction, editorial note following para. 62

## Significance

- Provides the authoritative, consultation-ready text of the VCC Principles, the version stakeholders and jurisdictions will comment on between July and September 2026.
- Expands the property architecture at Principle 5(1) to separate custodian and non-custodian creation scenarios, refining the creation-time snapshot rule found in the earlier ninth session draft.
- Names J.P. Morgan Kinexys as a live commercial example of registry-mirroring tokenisation in the Introduction's commentary, tying the instrument directly to an operating tokenised carbon market.
- Confirms the Tokenisation Annexe remains unresolved at the point of public consultation, leaving the treatment of on-chain VCCs as the single largest open drafting question in the project.
