# UNIDROIT Project on the Legal Nature of Verified Carbon Credits: Progress Update, 105th Governing Council Session

## Citation

UNIDROIT Secretariat. "Legal Nature of Verified Carbon Credits: Project Update". UNIDROIT Governing Council, 105th Session (C.D. (105) 10), Rome, 20-23 May 2025. https://www.unidroit.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/C.D.-105-10-Legal-Nature-of-Verified-Carbon-Credits.pdf.

## One-line summary

UNIDROIT's Working Group on Verified Carbon Credits has developed 24 draft Principles covering proprietary rights, transfer, custody, secured transactions, and insolvency, and is now incorporating tokenisation of VCCs as a discrete drafting item.

## Key claims

- The draft VCC Principles assert that VCCs can be the subject of proprietary rights because they satisfy four universal property attributes: they are individuated, controllable, rivalrous, and transferable.
- An innocent acquisition rule is included in the draft instrument because without it the nemo dat principle would deter secondary market participation in VCCs traded across multiple jurisdictions.
- Draft Principles 19 and 20 establish two pathways for third-party effectiveness of security rights in VCCs: registration in the VCC registry and execution of a control agreement.
- The draft Principles adopt a non-temporal priority rule giving registry-registration and control-agreement creditors priority over creditors who perfected by other means, regardless of timing.
- The fifth Working Group session in April 2025 addressed registry interoperability and tokenisation of VCCs as discrete topics affecting the draft instrument.
- The HCCH has mandated a new Experts' Group to develop an applicable law provision for VCCs, which UNIDROIT will incorporate as draft Principle 4.

## Excerpts

> "Draft Principle 3(1) asserts that VCCs can be the subject of proprietary rights. Indeed, there is general agreement that, to attract the necessary scale of investment needed for VCCs to raise climate finance, VCCs have to be deemed property."
> ~ Section IV, paragraph 17

> "The Working Group is in agreement that an innocent acquisition rule is needed to allow the market to scale. This is because in the absence of an innocent acquisition rule, the nemo dat rule would normally apply unexempted in the context of intangibles."
> ~ Section IV, paragraph 21

> "Draft Principle 21 addresses priority conflicts between secured creditors ... The draft Principle establishes a non-temporal priority rule: the secured creditor using the Principle 19 or Principle 20 methods will have priority even if these steps were taken after another creditor had already made its security right effective against third parties through registration or other means."
> ~ Section IV, paragraph 29

> "The Working Group discussed the topics of registry interoperability (including with respect to the transfer from one registry to another both within and outside of insolvency) and tokenisation, and considered the extent of their impact on the draft instrument."
> ~ Section V, paragraph 33

## Significance

- This is the primary 2025 source on UNIDROIT's developing private-law framework for tokenised carbon credits, which will become a binding soft-law instrument upon Governing Council adoption in 2026.
- The property-rights analysis (individualisation, control, rivalry, transferability) establishes a cross-jurisdictional doctrinal baseline for carbon credits that directly parallels the DAPL Principles' treatment of digital assets.
- The incorporation of tokenisation as a live drafting issue within the VCC Principles signals that the instrument will apply to on-chain as well as off-chain VCC markets.
- The non-temporal priority rule for secured transactions in VCCs is a material departure from most domestic secured-transactions frameworks and will affect collateral management for climate finance.
