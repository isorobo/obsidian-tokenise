# Draft UNIDROIT Principles on the Legal Nature of Verified Carbon Credits: Consultation Feedback

## Citation

UNIDROIT. "Draft UNIDROIT Principles on the Legal Nature of Verified Carbon Credits: Consultation Feedback". Study LXXXVI - W.G.10 - Doc. 3, Tenth session of the Working Group, Rome, 14-16 October 2026, 8 October 2026. https://www.unidroit.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Study-LXXXVI-W.G.10-Doc.-3-Public-Consultation-comments.pdf.

## One-line summary

UNIDROIT's compilation of public comments on the draft VCC Principles shows that respondents urged caution on tokenisation text and pressed for a clearer registry role, which shapes the Working Group's tenth-session revisions.

## Key claims

- The consultation ran from 13 July to 21 September 2026 and produced 32 submissions containing 476 comments, with comments from seven institutional observers and Consultative Committee appointees excluded from the count.
- Respondents were academics and think tanks (12), UNIDROIT Member States (10), carbon market participants (8) and intergovernmental organisations (2).
- Geographic spread was Europe 40.6%, Americas 25%, Asia Pacific 21.9%, MENA 6.25% and International 6.25%.
- On tokenisation, Luxembourg said the topic probably should not appear in the black-letter text, the World Federation of Exchanges advised considering it only once the liability structure is clear, and China called for restrictions.
- On registries, Germany wants the registry recognised as the mechanism for publicity and transfer, including privately operated registries, and Colombia stresses a broader public accounting and tracking function.
- Custody and insolvency drew little detailed comment, although Germany lists both among the core needs the instrument should cover.
- The document runs to about 410 pages and is a public Working Group paper for the tenth session; the Working Group and Consultative Committee comments (Docs. 4 and 5) stay confidential.

## Significance

- It is the first public evidence of how States and market participants view the bracketed tokenisation text in the draft VCC Principles, which links directly to the Tokenisation Annexe work.
- Registry comments bear on how private-law transfer and control rules interact with registry-based systems for carbon credits.
- Wording was read from a text proxy of the PDF because direct PDF extraction failed; the full 410 pages were not read, so details beyond the first section may differ.
- The document pairs with the consultation text and the ninth-session material already indexed.
