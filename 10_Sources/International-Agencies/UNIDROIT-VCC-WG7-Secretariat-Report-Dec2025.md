# UNIDROIT Working Group on the Legal Nature of Verified Carbon Credits: Secretariat's Report, Seventh Session

## Citation

UNIDROIT Secretariat. "Secretariat's Report". UNIDROIT Working Group on the Legal Nature of Verified Carbon Credits, Seventh Session (Study LXXXVI, W.G.7, Doc. 2 rev.), Rome, 17-19 December 2025. https://www.unidroit.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Study-LXXXVI-W.G.7-Doc.-2-rev.-Secretariats-Report.pdf.

## One-line summary

The Secretariat's report for the seventh Working Group session presents a consolidated 26-page account of the VCC Principles' development through six sessions, covering all nine sections of the 24-draft-Principle instrument, with tokenisation now a named topic for the eighth and final session.

## Key claims

- The Governing Council at its 105th session confirmed the VCC project for UNIDROIT's 2026-2028 Work Programme at high priority, with UNIDROIT General Assembly endorsement.
- The draft instrument covers nine sections addressing scope, applicable law, creation and transfer, cancellation, registry operations, custody, secured transactions, procedural enforcement, and insolvency.
- Tokenisation of VCCs is expressly named as a potential new draft Principle for the eighth session, scheduled for April 2026.
- The Working Group includes 13 international experts from 12 countries representing civil law, common law, and mixed jurisdictions, chaired by Professor Hideki Kanda of the University of Tokyo.
- The Consultative Committee established in August 2024 comprises 30 experts from 19 Member States, chaired by Singapore's Ministry of Law Director-General Sharon Ong.
- Cooperation with the HCCH on the choice-of-law provision and with UNCITRAL on secured transactions runs in parallel to the Working Group's substantive drafting.

## Excerpts

> "The Governing Council further recommended that the project on the legal nature of VCCs be maintained in the Institute's Work Programme for 2026-2028, with high priority, a recommendation which was endorsed by UNIDROIT's General Assembly."
> ~ Section I.A, paragraph 7

> "At this session, the Working Group is expected to consider: (i) a set of revised draft Principles; (ii) the written feedback of the Consultative Committee; and (iii) potential new draft Principles in relation to procedural law including enforcement and insolvency, as well as, possibly, tokenisation."
> ~ Section I.C.13

> "The draft VCC Principles cover a subset of private law issues relating to VCCs. They focus on proprietary rights and specifically where VCCs are the object of dispositions and acquisitions, and where rights and interests in VCCs are to be asserted against third parties."
> ~ Section II.B (citing Principle 1)

## Significance

- This is the most current (December 2025) consolidated primary record of the VCC Principles' drafting state, covering all 24 draft Principles and providing the pre-final-session baseline for the instrument.
- The explicit naming of tokenisation as a Principle-level agenda item for April 2026 establishes that on-chain VCC markets will receive dedicated treatment in the final instrument.
- The document tracks three parallel international law processes (HCCH, UNCITRAL, UNIDROIT) converging on carbon-credit private law, making it the key source for fragmentation risk analysis.
- Singapore's chairing of the Consultative Committee signals Asia-Pacific centrality to VCC market development and regulatory alignment.
