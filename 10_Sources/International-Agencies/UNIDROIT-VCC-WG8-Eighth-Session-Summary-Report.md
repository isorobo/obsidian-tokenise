# Summary Report of the Eighth Session of the UNIDROIT Working Group on the Legal Nature of Verified Carbon Credits

## Citation

UNIDROIT Secretariat. "Summary Report of the Eighth Session of the UNIDROIT Working Group on the Legal Nature of Verified Carbon Credits". Study LXXXVI – W.G.8 – Doc. 9, Rome, 15-17 April 2026. https://www.unidroit.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Study-LXXXVI-W.G.8-Doc.-9-Summary-Report.pdf.

## One-line summary

The eighth session summary records the Working Group's endorsement of Andrea Tosato's four-scenario tokenisation taxonomy and sets December 2026 as the target for Governing Council approval of the VCC Principles, independent of the still-developing Tokenisation Annexe.

## Key claims

- The Working Group generally agreed with the four-scenario tokenisation framework set out in the Tokenisation Annexe Memo (Study LXXXVI – W.G.8 – Doc. 6), covering technology substitution, contractual-rights tokens, proprietary-rights tokens, and disintermediated on-chain VCCs.
- Scenarios One and Two, permissioned DLT as registry infrastructure and tokens representing contractual rights, require no changes to the black-letter Principles and can be addressed through commentary alone.
- Scenario Three, tokens embodying proprietary rights transferable by transferring the token, would require a new substantive rule and an "operative" Tokenisation Annexe if the Working Group pursues it.
- Scenario Four, VCCs created and managed on a decentralised public ledger without an identifiable registry operator, falls outside the scope of the Principles because it removes the intermediaries the framework depends on.
- A World Bank representative stated that encoding a VCC's evidentiary record into a linked asset gives end users an auditable record alongside the asset, supporting full accounting for climate finance.
- Working Group participants noted that at least six, potentially ten, developing countries are adapting national registries in ways consistent with Scenario Three.
- The Secretary-General confirmed the Principles are targeted for December 2026 approval independent of tokenisation work, which will proceed in parallel and may not be complete by that date.

> ~ para. 266

## Significance

- Confirms the Working Group's institutional endorsement of Tosato's four-scenario taxonomy, moving it from an individual memo to an agreed analytical framework.
- Establishes that the core VCC Principles proceed to approval on a separate timeline from the Tokenisation Annexe, decoupling the property law framework from unresolved tokenisation questions.
- Records market evidence of national registry adaptation in six to ten developing countries, corroborating that tokenised VCC creation under Scenario Three is already underway.
- Documents World Bank institutional support for tokenisation as a tool for evidentiary accounting in climate finance markets.
