# The Macroeconomics of Stablecoins

## Citation

Hofmann, Boris, Matthias Kaldorf, and Matthias Rottner. "The Macroeconomics of Stablecoins". BIS Working Papers, No 1363, 23 June 2026. https://www.bis.org/publ/work1363.htm.

## One-line summary

A general equilibrium model calibrated to US data shows that stablecoin adoption modestly reduces long-run output because the bank lending channel outweighs the fiscal space channel.

## Key claims

- Stablecoins affect the economy through two opposing forces: a bank lending channel (household demand for stablecoins raises deposit rates, increases bank funding costs, and reduces loan supply) and a fiscal space channel (stablecoin issuers' demand for Treasury bills lowers sovereign borrowing costs).
- When calibrated to US data, the bank lending channel dominates, so widespread adoption produces a modest long-run reduction in output.
- Short-run effects remain positive, because the deposit rate increase attracts savings into the banking system before loan supply contracts fully.
- The net macroeconomic effect depends on regulatory design, the level of sovereign debt, and the degree of foreign demand for stablecoins.
- Reserve requirements on stablecoin issuers affect which channel dominates, making prudential regulation a lever for macroeconomic outcomes, not only for consumer protection.

## Significance

- Provides the first BIS general equilibrium treatment of the macroeconomic transmission of stablecoin adoption, linking prudential regulation to output and credit supply.
- Directly informs the wiki's stablecoin and monetary policy domain by quantifying the trade-off between sovereign borrowing cost reduction and bank credit contraction.
- The finding that reserve requirements alter which channel dominates strengthens the regulatory design literature on stablecoin reserve rules (compare MiCA, US GENIUS Act proposals).
- Complements WP 1355 ("Making stablecoins stable(r)") and WP 1359 ("The anatomy of stablecoin transactions") in the vault's BIS stablecoin trilogy.
