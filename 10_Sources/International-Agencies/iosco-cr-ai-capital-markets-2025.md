# Artificial Intelligence in Capital Markets: Use Cases, Risks, and Challenges

## Citation

IOSCO. "Artificial Intelligence in Capital Markets: Use Cases, Risks, and Challenges". IOSCO Consultation Report IOSCOPD788, 21 March 2025. https://www.iosco.org/library/pubdocs/pdf/IOSCOPD788.pdf.

## One-line summary

IOSCO's Fintech Task Force surveys how financial institutions deploy AI across capital markets, identifies five primary risk categories, and consults on whether existing securities regulatory frameworks are adequate or whether new AI-specific approaches are needed.

## Key claims

- Almost half (49%) of surveyed market participants have adopted AI capabilities, with 41% operating AI use cases in production environments.
- Primary AI use cases include algorithmic trading (63%), robo-advising and asset management (60%), and surveillance and fraud detection (53%).
- Five risk categories are primary: malicious uses of AI, model and data integrity risks, concentration risk from limited cloud and AI model providers, outsourcing and third-party dependence, and evolving governance gaps.
- Cybersecurity is the dominant concern among market participants (rated 4.26 out of 5), followed by data privacy and protection.
- Investors exposed to AI-enhanced scams invest 22% more money than those facing conventional fraudulent schemes.
- Regulatory responses vary: some jurisdictions extend existing frameworks to AI uses while others are developing new AI-specific regulatory approaches.

## Significance

- This is IOSCO's first cross-jurisdictional survey of AI deployment in capital markets and is foundational for the wiki's agentic-AI trading and AI governance domains.
- The algorithmic trading and robo-advising coverage maps directly to the wiki's llm-trading and agentic-ai domain threads.
- The concentration risk finding (few cloud and AI model providers) introduces a systemic dimension not covered in prior IOSCO digital-asset work.
- As a consultation report, it precedes a final IOSCO supervisory toolkit (FR/02/2026), making it a key waypoint in the regulatory timeline.
