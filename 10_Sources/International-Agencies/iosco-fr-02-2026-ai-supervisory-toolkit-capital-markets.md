# Supervisory Toolkit for AI Use in Capital Markets

## Citation

IOSCO Fintech Task Force. "Supervisory Toolkit for AI Use in Capital Markets". IOSCO Final Report FR/02/2026 (IOSCOPD823), 25 May 2026. https://www.iosco.org/library/pubdocs/pdf/IOSCOPD823.pdf.

## One-line summary

IOSCO provides securities regulators with a practical, non-prescriptive toolkit for supervising AI systems across their full lifecycle, covering traditional machine learning through to generative AI and agentic AI techniques.

## Key claims

- The toolkit applies to all AI system types used by regulated entities, from traditional machine learning models to generative AI and emerging agentic AI techniques.
- AI systems introduce or amplify risks related to complexity, reduced transparency, third-party dependencies, and governance challenges that existing supervisory frameworks must address.
- Supervisors should classify AI systems along a spectrum of risk and proportionality to determine what supervisory measures are appropriate for each system.
- AI-enabled cyber capabilities may materially accelerate threat evolution and increase the speed, scope, and scale of existing attack techniques against financial infrastructure.
- Regulators should consider requiring firms to provide meaningful information to clients about AI use that affects their outcomes.
- The outputs of AI systems are only as trustworthy as the data they consume, requiring firms to demonstrate sufficient data quality to prevent bias.
- Firms must maintain AI inventories, outcome logs, and explainability requirements for AI logic, plus business continuity planning for AI system failures.

## Significance

- IOSCO's first practical supervisory toolkit for AI extends regulatory oversight explicitly to agentic AI systems, directly addressing the wiki's agentic-AI trading domain.
- The risk-proportionality framework gives regulators a common methodology to assess AI systems used in securities markets, including algorithmic and autonomous trading.
- The toolkit coordinates with FSB work, signalling international regulatory convergence on AI governance in capital markets.
- By requiring explainability, outcome logging, and client disclosure, the toolkit sets the benchmark against which national regimes for AI in finance will be assessed.
