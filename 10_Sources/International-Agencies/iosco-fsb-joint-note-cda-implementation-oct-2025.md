# Progress towards Implementing Comprehensive Policy Frameworks for Crypto-Asset and Stablecoin Activities: FSB and IOSCO Joint Information Note

## Citation

IOSCO; FSB. "Progress towards Implementing Comprehensive Policy Frameworks for Crypto-Asset and Stablecoin Activities: FSB and IOSCO Joint Information Note". IOSCO-FSB Joint Publication, 16 October 2025. https://www.iosco.org/library/pubdocs/pdf/IOSCOPD801-joint-note.pdf.

## One-line summary

The FSB and IOSCO jointly summarise progress and gaps from their complementary October 2025 thematic reviews, finding that global implementation of crypto-asset and stablecoin regulatory frameworks remains uneven and fragmented despite meaningful advances in several jurisdictions.

## Key claims

- The note accompanies two distinct but complementary thematic reviews: IOSCO's FR/13/25 (investor protection and market integrity) and the FSB's parallel review (financial stability and stablecoin framework).
- Regulatory progress is uneven and fragmented at the global level; some jurisdictions such as the EU (MiCA) have mature frameworks while others have limited or insufficient approaches.
- The FSB flagged specific risks from multi-issuer stablecoin models and called for regulatory frameworks to be as comprehensive and robust as possible.
- Both bodies urged jurisdictions to accelerate adoption of their respective recommendations and strengthen cross-border co-operation to prevent regulatory arbitrage.
- Stablecoin implementation received separate FSB-led treatment, with IOSCO's review focusing on investor protection and market integrity rather than the stablecoin subset.

## Significance

- This is the primary joint statement from the two lead international standard-setters on crypto-asset regulatory progress and is foundational for the wiki's cross-jurisdictional coverage.
- The stablecoin-specific findings from the FSB component directly inform the wiki's stablecoin and reserve-requirements analysis.
- The divergence between EU (MiCA) and other jurisdictions, documented here, is load-bearing for the wiki's comparative law and regulatory arbitrage threads.
- The joint framing confirms that FSB (financial stability) and IOSCO (investor protection) constitute the two-pillar international regulatory architecture for digital assets.
