# GENIUS Act Regulations: Final Rule

## Citation

Office of the Comptroller of the Currency. "GENIUS Act Regulations: Final Rule". OCC Bulletins (Bulletin 2026-50), 21 October 2026. https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2026/bulletin-2026-50.html.

## One-line summary

The OCC finalised most of its GENIUS Act rules for payment stablecoin issuers in a new 12 CFR Part 15, effective 18 January 2027, leaving Bank Secrecy Act, AML and sanctions rules for a separate Treasury-coordinated rulemaking.

## Key claims

- The OCC issued a final rule implementing the GENIUS Act for payment stablecoin issuance and related activities by entities under its jurisdiction.
- The rule covers national banks, federal savings associations, federal branches and agencies and their subsidiaries, foreign payment stablecoin issuers, nonbank federal qualified issuers, and state qualified issuers under OCC authority.
- A new 12 CFR Part 15 holds most of the rules: activities, reserve assets, redemption, risk management, audits and reporting, custody, and licensing for permitted issuers.
- Further provisions address state issuers moving to the federal framework, supervision of foreign issuers, revocation of permitted issuer approval, and capital and operational backstops.
- The rule amends existing OCC regulations on capital (12 CFR 3), prompt corrective action (12 CFR 6), assessment fees (12 CFR 8) and rules of practice (12 CFR 19).
- Bank Secrecy Act, anti-money laundering and OFAC sanctions rules are deferred to a separate rulemaking in coordination with Treasury.
- The final rule takes effect on 18 January 2027.

## Significance

- This is the primary OCC rule text pointer for the US payment stablecoin licensing regime, so it anchors the vault's GENIUS Act implementation coverage.
- It moves the vault from statute-level coverage to operative regulation, including reserve, redemption and custody requirements.
- The AML and sanctions carve-out signals a further Treasury and OCC rule still to come.
