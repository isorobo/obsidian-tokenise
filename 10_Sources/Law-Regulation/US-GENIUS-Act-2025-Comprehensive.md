# GENIUS Act of 2025: Comprehensive Federal Stablecoin Regulation

## Citation

United States Congress, Senate Bill 1582 (119th Congress). "Guiding and Establishing National Innovation for U.S. Stablecoins Act." Signed into law July 18, 2025. https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/1582

## Legislative Timeline

- **Introduced**: May 21, 2025 by Senator Bill Hagerty (R-TN)
- **Senate passed**: June 17, 2025 (68–30 bipartisan vote)
- **House passed**: July 17, 2025 (308–122)
- **Signed into law**: July 18, 2025 by President Donald Trump

## One-line Summary

The first comprehensive federal regulatory framework for payment stablecoins, establishing 1:1 reserve-backing requirements, three-tier issuer licensing, and federal preemption of state stablecoin regulation, effective 18 months after enactment (January 2027) or 120 days after implementing regulations, whichever is earlier.

---

## Key Definitions

### Payment Stablecoin
A digital asset designed to maintain stable value relative to a fixed monetary amount and redeemable at that fixed value by the issuer. Explicitly excludes:
- National currencies
- Bank deposits
- Securities
- Commodities

### Permitted Issuer Categories

The Act establishes three lawful issuer categories:

1. **Insured Depository Institution (IDI) Subsidiaries**
   - Banks, credit unions, and thrifts licensed under banking law
   - Automatic compliance (no separate stablecoin approval required)

2. **Federal Qualified Payment Stablecoin Issuers**
   - Nonbank entities approved by the Federal Reserve
   - OR uninsured national banks chartered by the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC)
   - No preexisting geographic or activity constraints
   - Subject to Federal Reserve prudential oversight

3. **State Qualified Payment Stablecoin Issuers**
   - Entities regulated under state law with certified "substantially similar" frameworks
   - Issuance cap: $10 billion outstanding stablecoins
   - Exceeding cap triggers mandatory transition to federal oversight within 360 days
   - Early-stage issuers with established frameworks eligible for presumptive waiver approval

**Prohibited issuers**: Crypto exchanges, private equity firms, tech platforms, non-financial public companies (without unanimous Committee approval)

## Core Regulatory Requirements

### 1. Reserve Backing (1:1 Requirement)

Every stablecoin must maintain reserves equal to circulation in high-quality liquid assets:

**Eligible reserves:**
- U.S. currency (coins and bills)
- Treasury securities (duration <93 days maturity)
- Demand deposits at insured depository institutions (FDIC coverage applies)
- Repurchase agreements secured by eligible Treasury bills
- Government money market funds
- Central bank reserves (held at Federal Reserve Banks)
- Tokenised federal government assets (subject to regulator approval)

**Reserve management:**
- Segregated accounts (non-rehypothecated)
- Monthly attestation by third-party auditor
- Quarterly public disclosure of reserve composition
- Annual audited financial statements for issuers exceeding $50 billion circulation

### 2. Redemption and Conversion

- Issuers must redeem stablecoins at stated par value for fiat currency
- Redemption must occur within **seven days** of request
- All fees associated with purchasing or redeeming stablecoins must be publicly disclosed in advance
- Seven days' notice required for any change to fee structure or redemption policy

### 3. Operational Constraints

- **No yield payments**: Stablecoin holders cannot earn interest or returns
- **Narrow business scope**: Issuers limited to issuing, redeeming, and managing reserves; cannot offer other financial services tied to stablecoin holdings
- **No tying restrictions**: Cannot condition stablecoin services on purchase of unrelated products or services
- **Bank Secrecy Act compliance**: Full AML/CFT obligations apply; customer identification programs (CIP), suspicious-activity reporting (SAR), sanctions-list verification

### 4. Bankruptcy Protections

The Act amends the U.S. Bankruptcy Code to provide stablecoin holders with:
- **Reserve exclusion**: Required reserves are not part of the issuer's bankruptcy estate
- **Super-priority claims**: Stablecoin holders receive priority above administrative expenses if reserves prove insufficient

This protects customer assets from general creditor claims in insolvency.

## Regulatory Architecture

### Dual-Track Regulation

**Federal pathway (primary)**:
- Federal Reserve supervises nonbank federal-qualified issuers
- OCC supervises uninsured national banks issuing stablecoins
- FDIC supervises insured depository institution subsidiaries
- Treasury coordinates interagency implementation

**State pathway (limited)**:
- Issuers with <$10 billion outstanding may opt for state regulation
- State frameworks must be certified "substantially similar" to federal standards by a Stablecoin Certification Review Committee (Treasury, Federal Reserve, FDIC)
- State-qualified issuers exceeding $10 billion must transition to federal oversight within 360 days

### Stablecoin Certification Review Committee

Roles:
- Certify state regulatory frameworks as substantially similar to federal standards
- Grant waivers or extensions for state-qualified issuers exceeding $10 billion cap
- Review applications from foreign stablecoin providers
- Advise on regulatory interoperability

Composition: Treasury (chair), Federal Reserve, FDIC

### Federal Preemption

- The Act preempts state licensing requirements for federal-qualified issuers and IDI subsidiaries
- State-qualified issuers receive reciprocal treatment: host states cannot apply requirements stricter than those on federal issuers
- No state can impose conflicting reserve or disclosure requirements on compliant issuers

## Foreign Stablecoin Providers

Foreign entities may access U.S. markets if they:

1. **Regulatory equivalence**: Operate under Treasury-certified comparable regulatory regimes
2. **OCC registration**: Register with the OCC (30-day approval timeline)
3. **U.S. reserves**: Maintain U.S. deposits or Treasury holdings for customer liquidity on U.S. soil
4. **Non-sanctioned status**: Operate from non-sanctioned jurisdictions; issuance not prohibited under U.S. law
5. **AML/CFT compliance**: Full Bank Secrecy Act and sanctions obligations

**Implementation note**: Foreign stablecoin issuers (e.g., Tether as a BVI company) must satisfy OCC registration and U.S. reserve-holding requirements or cease U.S. settlement.

---

## Non-Financial Public Companies

Publicly traded companies outside the financial sector face an **exceptionally high bar** for stablecoin issuance:

- **Unanimous approval** required from the Stablecoin Certification Review Committee
- Committee must verify:
  - No systemic risk posed by the issuer
  - Full compliance with data-usage limitations
  - No conflicts with fiduciary duties to shareholders
  - Adequate technical and operational competence

**Effect**: Effectively prohibits tech companies (e.g., Meta, Amazon) and other non-financial public firms from issuing stablecoins.

## Interoperability Standards

Federal regulators must establish technical standards promoting compatibility between:
- Different stablecoin issuers
- Stablecoin platforms and the broader digital-finance ecosystem
- Private blockchains and public infrastructure
- Legacy payment systems and DLT settlement

## Impact on Incumbents

### USDC (Circle)
- **Status**: Likely approved as federal-qualified nonbank issuer
- **Reasoning**: Already MiCA-compliant (EU); transparent reserve reporting; FDIC-insured backing
- **Compliance cost**: Minimal; already operating under comparable standards

### USDT (Tether)
- **Status**: Uncertain; faces major compliance hurdles
- **Reasoning**: Foreign issuer (BVI); OCC registration required; U.S. reserve requirements impose operational burden
- **Adaptation**: Tether pivoted with USAT launch (January 2026) via Anchorage Digital Bank subsidiary to satisfy domestic-issuance requirements

### JPMorgan Coin
- **Status**: Automatically compliant (bank-issued)
- **Reasoning**: JPMorgan is an insured depository institution; subsidiary issuance requires no separate approval

### Other stablecoins
- Non-approved stablecoins face settlement uncertainty in U.S. post-2027
- Market likely consolidates around 3–5 approved issuers

---

## Market Implications

### Short-term (2026–2027)
- Fed finalizes non-bank issuer approval process
- Circle and other nonbank entities seek federal qualification
- Tether and non-compliant issuers exit U.S. retail markets or seek waivers
- IDI subsidiaries launch competing stablecoins

### Medium-term (2027–2028)
- Only approved stablecoins settle on U.S. blockchain infrastructure
- USDC market share consolidates; USDT relegated to offshore trading
- Bank-issued stablecoins (JPM Coin, SocGen EURCV) capture institutional settlement demand
- Retail stablecoin market narrows to 3–5 issuers

### Long-term (2028+)
- Fed wholesale CBDC (if launched) competes directly with bank-issued stablecoins
- Retail stablecoin market stabilizes around government-backed and bank-issued instruments
- Unregulated stablecoins trade only in jurisdictions outside U.S. regulatory reach

## Significance

- **Historic**: First federal U.S. stablecoin statute, replacing fragmented state guidance and OCC guidance
- **Three-tier architecture**: Creates tiered issuance categories (banks, federal qualified, state qualified), enabling regulatory scalability
- **Market consolidation**: Effectively limits U.S. stablecoin supply to pre-approved issuers; accelerates market consolidation around 3–5 leaders
- **Reserve certainty**: 1:1 requirement with eligible-asset list provides institutional confidence in stablecoin backing
- **Monetary-policy impact**: Federal Reserve gains real-time visibility into stablecoin circulation; coordinates with Treasury on macro implications
- **International competitiveness**: Establishes U.S. as jurisdiction with clearest regulatory framework for dollar stablecoins, potentially attracting global issuance
