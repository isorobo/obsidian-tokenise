# From Commodity to Fiat and Now to Crypto: What Does History Tell Us?

## Citation

Eichengreen, Barry. "From Commodity to Fiat and Now to Crypto: What Does History Tell Us?". NBER Working Paper 25426, January 2019 (the page gives the month only, so 1 January is a placeholder day). https://www.nber.org/papers/w25426. DOI 10.3386/w25426.

## One-line summary

Eichengreen reads monetary history as a pull toward one currency per jurisdiction, doubts that crypto-assets or stablecoins can scale and stay stable, and sees a central bank digital currency as the one option that clearly meets the challenge.

## Key claims

- Currencies have historically tended to converge toward a single currency per jurisdiction.
- Control over seigniorage is a source of political power.
- A uniform currency free of counterparty and liquidity risk supports economic activity.
- Digital currencies could reverse the trend toward one currency per jurisdiction, but their varying prices suggest they do not yet perform the core functions of money.
- Stablecoins aim to close that gap, but their ability to scale and remain stable is doubtful.
- Central bank digital currency is the one option that clearly meets these challenges, though it carries costs as well as benefits.
