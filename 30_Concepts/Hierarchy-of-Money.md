As at 27 October 2026 no vault source note records the word "hierarchy" in its verbatim excerpts: [[Gabor-Vestergaard-Towards-Theory-Shadow-Money-2016]] carries it in the vault's own key-claim summary, [[Murau-Van-Klooster-Rethinking-Monetary-Sovereignty-2022]] only in its tag and annotation, and every other source only bears on the idea, so check the "Basis" column before attributing the term to any author. # Hierarchy of money

> **Definition.** Working summary, not a quoted definition: the hierarchy of money is the idea that forms of money are ranked by how widely they are accepted and whether they convert at par into a higher form, with central bank money at the top, bank deposits below it and other private money below that [V1].

## Why it matters

The vault's stablecoin, tokenised-deposit and CBDC sources keep returning to how new private money relates to central bank money and bank deposits: whether it converts at par, who can redeem it, what backs it and whether its issuer can reach central bank reserves. Gabor and Vestergaard supply the vault's one theory statement of the idea, which the vault note summarises as a hierarchy in which "the test of moneyness is convertibility at par into a higher layer" (2016, INET) [[Gabor-Vestergaard-Towards-Theory-Shadow-Money-2016]]. Murau and van 't Klooster divide money into public money, regulated private-public money and private money, and state that most money is private credit money (2022, Perspectives on Politics) [[Murau-Van-Klooster-Rethinking-Monetary-Sovereignty-2022]]. Central bank and IMF sources bear on the same ranking without using the word: a Bank of England staff model treats reserves and cash as the stabilisers of singleness (as of 2026-02, BoE) [[BoE-SWP1174-Model-of-Monetary-Singleness]]; the Bank's Deputy Governor says "we have a low risk appetite for a significant shift away from settlement in central bank money." (as of 2026-05, BoE) [[BoE-Breeden-Modernising-Money-and-Markets-2026]]; and the IMF names as a central policy question "the extent to which stablecoin issuers should have access to central bank reserves" (as of 2026-07, IMF) [[IMF-Note-2026-006-Rise-of-Tokenization]]. The concept sits next to [[Singleness-of-Money]], which asks whether the forms trade one for one, and anchors the [[MOC - Monetary-Hierarchy]], whose coverage-gap section recorded no hierarchy-of-money source as at 24 October 2026; the two theory notes cited above were added to the vault on 27 October 2026.

## Open points

- **No foundational texts.** The vault does not yet hold the foundational hierarchy-of-money texts, so the definition above is a working summary [V1] and `defined_in` is not set. The [[MOC - Monetary-Hierarchy]] coverage-gap section recorded no hierarchy source as at 24 October 2026; theory sources have to be added by hand.
- **Word versus vault summary.** The only place the vault attributes the word "hierarchy" to an author is the key-claim summary in [[Gabor-Vestergaard-Towards-Theory-Shadow-Money-2016]]; the paper excerpts recorded there concern the definition and procyclicality of shadow money, and the paper was read through a text proxy [V2]. [[Murau-Van-Klooster-Rethinking-Monetary-Sovereignty-2022]] carries a `hierarchy-of-money` tag, but its key claims describe three governance segments, not a ranking [V3].
- **Is a stablecoin money at all.** [[IMF-Note-2026-006-Rise-of-Tokenization]] says stablecoins do not appear to be money (a contractual test); [[Gorton-Zhang-Taming-Wildcat-Stablecoins-2023]] treats them as circulating private money that is subpar in acceptance (a functional test); [[FEDS-Note-2026-09-New-Forms-Money-US-Monetary-Aggregates]] considers them for the monetary aggregates (a statistical test). These answer different questions and are not a direct exchange.
- **What secures convertibility upward.** The vault holds several candidate mechanisms without a settled view: collateral market liquidity ([[Gabor-Vestergaard-Towards-Theory-Shadow-Money-2016]]), breadth of redemption access ([[FEDS-Note-2026-02-Brief-History-Bank-Notes-Stablecoins]]), reserves and cash ([[BoE-SWP1174-Model-of-Monetary-Singleness]]), and backing-asset rules with a liquidity facility ([[BoE-Sterling-Denominated-Systemic-Stablecoins-Policy-Statement-2026]]). [[FEDS-2026-037-Fragility-Perfectly-Safe-Digital-Money]] models runs that occur even with safe reserves. The same list appears in [[Singleness-of-Money]].
- **Across currencies.** [[BIS-WP1370-Dollarisation-Monetary-Control-Stablecoins]], [[NYFed-SR1202-Stablecoins-Meet-Mundell-Fleming-Trilemma]] and [[BIS-WP291-US-Dollar-Shortage-Global-Banking-Policy-Response]] deal with access to dollars across borders. Some accounts extend the hierarchy across currencies, with the US dollar at the top [V4]; none of these vault notes frames its findings that way.
- **Annotation links only.** Several notes connect to the monetary-hierarchy thread only through the vault's Significance annotations, not through source text: [[NYFed-SR1202-Stablecoins-Meet-Mundell-Fleming-Trilemma]], [[BoE-Sterling-Denominated-Systemic-Stablecoins-Policy-Statement-2026]], [[BoE-Breeden-Modernising-Money-and-Markets-2026]], [[ECB-Blog-2026-Digital-Euro-Opportunity-for-Banks]], [[BoE-SWP1174-Model-of-Monetary-Singleness]], [[FSB-Global-Stablecoin-Arrangements-Cross-Border-Vulnerabilities-2026]] and [[NYFed-LSE-Stablecoins-NonCrypto-Shocks-2026-Update]]. The annotation in [[Nakamoto-Bitcoin-Peer-to-Peer-Electronic-Cash-System-2008]] names it as a text against which the hierarchy sources can be read; the white paper itself does not discuss a hierarchy of money.
- **Partial reads.** [[Eichengreen-NBER-WP25426-Commodity-Fiat-Crypto-2019]] and [[BIS-WP291-US-Dollar-Shortage-Global-Banking-Policy-Response]] rest on abstracts; [[Gorton-Zhang-Taming-Wildcat-Stablecoins-2023]] was read through a text proxy covering about the first 100,000 of 140,000 characters.
